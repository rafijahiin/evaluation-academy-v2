<Callout variant="key" title="Two interrelated approaches in Phase 5">
**Strategic communication:** a purposeful, planned approach to communicating CPE results in a timely and targeted manner — so evaluative evidence proactively informs decisions, programmes, and policies.

**Facilitation of use:** deliberate steps to engage stakeholders over the long term and advocate for use of the results — including the management response and follow-up on its implementation.
</Callout>

Phase 5 is what turns the report from a published document into actual programme change.

<h3>The joint ownership map</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="CPE Manager + CO Communication Officer">**Jointly responsible** for strategic communication on the CPE. They draft, refine, and execute the communication plan. They commission and review products. They time the dissemination around decision-making windows.</KV>

  <KV title="Evaluation team — inputs and products">Provide the CPE Manager key inputs during communication planning — identifying audiences and context, defining the CPE key messages. They develop some communication products (presentations, photographs from data collection). They participate in face-to-face and remote events such as dissemination workshops, webinars, advocacy meetings.</KV>

  <KV title="ERG members">Consulted on the communication plan during its development. Continue to be advocates for CPE results within their networks during dissemination.</KV>

  <KV title="Regional M&E Adviser + programme experts">Consulted during communication planning. The RO M&E Adviser also quality-assures the management response (lesson 5.3.1).</KV>

  <KV title="Senior management + technical staff">Play a central role in **increasing the uptake** of the evaluation. Their support is what gets the management response taken seriously and acted on across the next programme cycle.</KV>

  <KV title="CO Representative">Approves the communication plan before implementation. Approves the management response. Their public endorsement signals to stakeholders that the CPE is being treated as a real input to programme adaptation.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>What the CPE Manager carries</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Communication plan stewardship">Drafting and finalising the strategic communication plan (lesson 5.2.2).</KV>

  <KV title="Stakeholder consultation">With the evaluation team, ERG, RO M&E Adviser, and programme experts as the plan evolves.</KV>

  <KV title="Management response support">Coordinating, drafting, quality-assuring (with RO M&E Adviser), and submitting the management response.</KV>

  <KV title="Long-term facilitation of use">Pursuing new opportunities to apply CPE results well after release.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<InPractice country="The hardest role to staff well">
The CO communication officer is often a generalist with many other portfolios — country programme launches, fundraising, media liaison, internal comms. The CPE Manager has to bring them into the evaluation early — during the design phase — and protect time on their calendar for the dissemination phase. Communication that surges in the last two weeks before publication looks rushed and lands flat. The handbook expects the joint ownership to be built across the whole evaluation, not bolted on at the end.
</InPractice>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Strategic communication">A purposeful, planned approach to communicating CPE results so they inform decisions — not just publishing the report and hoping.</Term>
  <Term name="Facilitation of use">The deliberate engagement of stakeholders that turns CPE results into programmatic and policy change.</Term>
  <Term name="Joint ownership">The pattern of dual responsibility between the CPE Manager and the CO communication officer, with the evaluation team, ERG, and senior management contributing distinct roles.</Term>
  <Term name="Uptake">The extent to which CPE results actually inform decisions — the ultimate measure of Phase 5 success.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
