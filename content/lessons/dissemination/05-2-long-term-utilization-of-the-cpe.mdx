<Callout variant="key" title="Why long-term matters">
Beyond the immediate release and communication, the CPE Manager **cultivates and proactively sustains an advocacy approach in the long term** — to leverage the CPE for accelerating UNFPA's transformative results. The aim: integrate the evaluation results into decisions, programmes, strategies and policies, **going beyond the management response actions**. This is what maximises the value of the CPE.
</Callout>

Long-term utilisation depends on **senior management, technical staff, and communication staff supporting the CPE Manager**. Four practices form the spine of the work.

## Practice 1 — Identify new opportunities for CPE uptake

The CPE Manager continuously liaises with CO technical staff and country programme stakeholders — government, implementing partners — to surface new opportunities where CPE results can inform decisions or address emerging knowledge needs.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Internal moments">Mid-year review meetings; CO staff meetings; implementing partner reviews; donor meetings.</KV>

  <KV title="UN system moments">UNSDCF meetings; joint programme reviews; thematic UN community sessions.</KV>

  <KV title="Government moments">National policy revisions; sectoral working groups; legislative cycles.</KV>

  <KV title="Humanitarian moments">In CPEs with humanitarian components, the report often becomes a key source for **Inter-Agency Humanitarian Evaluations (IAHEs)** or the development of **Humanitarian Response Plans (HRPs)**.</KV>
</KVGrid>

## Practice 2 — Engage stakeholders proactively and continuously

Stakeholder engagement isn't a one-time event at dissemination. After release, the CPE Manager continues to engage and **provide ongoing support to stakeholders in using the evaluation results** — including in management response implementation.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Regular check-ins">Meetings with stakeholders to track how they're using the CPE results in their own planning.</KV>

  <KV title="Follow-up workshops">Bringing partners back together to discuss implications for decision-making, planning, and programming.</KV>

  <KV title="Webinars">Lower-cost, broader-reach forums to keep CPE findings present in stakeholder conversations.</KV>

  <KV title="Focus groups">Where deeper, more nuanced reaction is needed — e.g. on a contentious recommendation.</KV>

  <KV title="Youth engagement">The CPE Manager identifies meaningful ways to engage young people in long-term uptake — consistent with the leveraging-youth-in-evaluation agenda.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<Callout variant="tip" title="Why collaborative discussion matters">
Collaborative discussions lead to better stakeholder understanding of the evaluation results — and increase the likelihood the results actually inform decisions. CPE results that are "released" but not subsequently discussed often fail to influence the cycles they were meant to inform.
</Callout>

## Practice 3 — Use the CPE as a powerful source of content

The CPE Manager and Communication Officer **consistently leverage the CPE** as a source of content for the CO's wider communications work — focused on learning and accountability.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Donor reports">CPE findings substantiate progress claims and inform resource asks.</KV>

  <KV title="Project proposals">CPE evidence builds the case for new programme components.</KV>

  <KV title="International advocacy days">Commemorations of World Population Day, International Women's Day, Human Rights Day — opportunities to surface CPE findings in public discourse.</KV>

  <KV title="Senior management speeches">CO Representative, Deputy Representative — speeches drawing on CPE evidence give the CPE durable rhetorical weight.</KV>

  <KV title="Social media outreach">Findings reframed for public-facing audiences.</KV>

  <KV title="Joint UN communications">Where the CPE intersects with system-wide work — e.g. SDG progress reporting, Resident Coordinator briefings.</KV>
</KVGrid>

By leveraging the CPE in wider communications, the CO showcases its commitment to **transparency, learning, and accountability**.

## Practice 4 — Build an evaluation culture

After the communication plan has been fully implemented, the CPE Manager sustains efforts to communicate evaluation results **beyond the immediate country programme stakeholders** — promoting broader learning and programme adaptation.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Awareness raising">Seek opportunities to surface the significance of the CPE for learning, acceleration, and accountability.</KV>

  <KV title="Highlighting evaluation benefits">Demonstrate the practical value of evaluation findings to staff, partners, and decision-makers — making the case for evaluation as a programmatic investment, not a compliance task.</KV>

  <KV title="Sharing lessons widely">Share evaluation lessons on process and outcomes — through the M&E Net community, regional M&E forums, and beyond.</KV>

  <KV title="Mentoring future YEEs">Long-term utilisation includes investing in the next generation of evaluators by sharing what worked and what didn't in the CPE process.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<InPractice country="The 5-year horizon">
The handbook anchors long-term utilisation at **up to 5 years after the evaluation year** — which is when the management response can be closed (lesson 5.3.1). That same horizon is the period across which the CPE Manager keeps pulling findings forward into new opportunities. A CPE released in year 1 of a programme cycle should still be informing decisions in year 5 of the next cycle.
</InPractice>

<Checklist storageKey="ch5-l5-long-term" title="Long-term utilisation — annual review checklist">
  <Item>Are CPE findings being cited or referenced in this year's CO annual report and internal monitoring?</Item>
  <Item>Has the CPE been used as a source in at least one donor report or proposal in the last 12 months?</Item>
  <Item>Have any new opportunities for uptake (government policy revisions, donor meetings, joint programme reviews) been identified and acted on?</Item>
  <Item>Are stakeholders being re-engaged through follow-up workshops, webinars, or focus groups?</Item>
  <Item>Has the CPE Manager identified and supported youth engagement in CPE results uptake?</Item>
  <Item>Are evaluation lessons being shared through M&E Net community and regional M&E forums?</Item>
  <Item>Is the management response on track in TeamCentral — with twice-yearly reporting at minimum?</Item>
  <Item>Is the CO showcasing transparency, learning, and accountability through CPE-derived content in wider communications?</Item>
</Checklist>

<Callout variant="key" title="The endpoint of the journey">
A well-run CPE doesn't end with a published report. It ends — sometimes years later — when the next country programme is designed with the previous CPE's evidence visibly shaping the choices. That's what the five phases together are for. Preparation gave the CPE its scaffolding; design gave it its analytical chain; fieldwork gave it data; reporting gave it findings, conclusions, and recommendations; **dissemination gives it consequences**.
</Callout>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Long-term utilisation">The deliberate practice of pulling CPE findings forward — into decisions, programmes, donor reports, advocacy moments — for up to 5 years after the evaluation year.</Term>
  <Term name="IAHE">Inter-Agency Humanitarian Evaluation — a system-wide UN evaluation; CPEs with humanitarian components often feed into IAHE evidence.</Term>
  <Term name="HRP">Humanitarian Response Plan — the country-level plan for humanitarian action; CPE findings can inform HRP development.</Term>
  <Term name="Evaluation culture">The institutional disposition — within the CO and its partners — that treats evaluation as a programmatic investment and a learning tool, not a compliance burden.</Term>
  <Term name="M&E Net community">UNFPA's internal M&E community of practice — where evaluation lessons across countries are shared.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
