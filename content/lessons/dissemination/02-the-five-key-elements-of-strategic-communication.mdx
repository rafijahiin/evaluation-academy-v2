<Callout variant="key" title="Why these five elements">
Communication on CPE results is most effective when it's strategically built into the evaluation from the outset — not bolted on at publication. The five elements run sequentially: early planning, audience focus, product diversification, a minimum package, and timing aligned with decision-making cycles.
</Callout>

## Element 1 — Early planning

Communication elements are included in the **CPE ToR** (preparation phase). The initial communication plan is jointly developed by the CPE Manager and CO Communication Officer during the **design phase**, with input from thematic experts, the ERG, the evaluation team, and the RO M&E Adviser. The plan is **continuously refined** during the field and reporting phases, and **finalised and implemented** during dissemination.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Preparation phase">ToR includes communication and dissemination components.</KV>

  <KV title="Design phase">Initial communication plan drafted with stakeholder input.</KV>

  <KV title="Field phase">Plan updated as new advocacy and communication opportunities surface.</KV>

  <KV title="Reporting phase">Plan refined as ERG meeting and recommendations workshop reveal new audiences.</KV>

  <KV title="Dissemination phase">Plan finalised, approved by CO Representative, and executed.</KV>
</KVGrid>

## Element 2 — Audience-focused approach

The team must **identify internal and external audiences early** and reflect on how each will use the results — and for what purpose. The **stakeholder map** completed at the design phase (lesson 1.9) is the starting point.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Internal audiences">CO senior management, programme officers, technical specialists, the UNFPA Regional Office.</KV>

  <KV title="Government and policymakers">Often the most important external audience — prioritise an evaluation brief, fact sheet, executive summary, presentation.</KV>

  <KV title="Implementing partners and donors">May prefer the full evaluation report plus concise summary materials (executive summary, brief, presentation, video).</KV>

  <KV title="Other UN agencies and development partners">For accountability and coordination — often interested in thematic findings relevant to joint programming.</KV>

  <KV title="Civil society and academia">For independent assessment, peer review, and learning.</KV>

  <KV title="Rights-holders and general public">Reached through social media, radio, local TV, posters and pamphlets in local languages, local newspapers — channels matching the audience's information environment.</KV>
</KVGrid>

For each audience, the CPE Manager and Communication Officer determine the **most suitable channels and platforms**. Same results — different packaging.

## Element 3 — Relevant, innovative, diversified products

Diversifying products doesn't mean altering results — it means presenting them in **clear, jargon-free language**, contextualised to the audience's knowledge needs while retaining a **balanced and nuanced** presentation of what works, what doesn't, why, for whom, and under what circumstances.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Substance unchanged, format adapted">
A video and a research-quality executive summary can carry the same conclusions about a country programme. The team's judgement is preserved across formats — what changes is depth, length, and channel. Never water down the analysis to make it palatable.
</Callout>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="For senior management and policymakers">Evaluation brief; fact sheet; executive summary; presentation — quick access to key messages.</KV>

  <KV title="For programme officers, technical specialists, IPs, donors">Full evaluation report plus the concise materials.</KV>

  <KV title="For rights-holders, local stakeholders, general public">Social media; radio; local TV broadcasts; posters and pamphlets in local languages; local newspapers.</KV>

  <KV title="For all audiences">A brief video — in national and local languages — of the evaluation highlights as a quick audio-visual summary.</KV>

  <KV title="For inclusive access">Evaluation brief translated into national and local languages.</KV>

  <KV title="For internal UNFPA audiences">Email messages; webinars; posts on MyUNFPA intranet; M&E Net community and other thematic communities.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<Callout variant="tip" title="Long-term accessibility">
CPE products must be **available through several channels and remain easily accessible for future use**. Publish on the CO website, the UNFPA evaluation database, the M&E Net community, and external/internal repositories. Place all CPE products in an all-staff accessible folder on the CO Google Drive.
</Callout>

## Element 4 — A minimum communications package

To balance standardisation with context, every CPE develops a **minimum communications package** — a set of core obligatory products supplemented by optional products tailored to context.

Developing the minimum package is **mandatory**. All products should be **released simultaneously** and in a timely manner.

### Figure 7 — The minimum package

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Obligatory products">**Evaluation report** · **Executive summary** (situated in the report) · **Brief** (national + local languages) · **Case studies** (as relevant) · **Video** · **Presentation**</KV>

  <KV title="Optional products">**Webinar** · **Email and community announcement** · **Website feature** · **Social media** · **Thematic briefs and notes** · **Interactive snapshots** · **Podcasts** · **Blogs** · **Photo blogs**</KV>
</KVGrid>

<InPractice country="Why the “minimum” matters">
Without a minimum, every CPE's dissemination depends on the energy of that particular CO and that particular communication officer. With a minimum, every CPE — Bangladesh, Burkina Faso, Bolivia — produces at least the same core set of materials. The audiences can rely on what to expect. The cross-country comparability lifts.
</InPractice>

## Element 5 — Timing for decision-making

To facilitate use, the team must be **timely** and aware of key decision-making cycles inside and outside UNFPA.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="UNFPA programming cycle">The CPE report should be released **in time to feed into the preparation of the next programming cycle** — informing the white paper and the new CPD.</KV>

  <KV title="National policy windows">Aligning release and communication with a national policy development or revision process can substantially increase uptake.</KV>

  <KV title="Advocacy days and events">International Women's Day; World Population Day; 16 Days of Activism Against GBV; Human Rights Day; national and regional days relevant to UNFPA's mandate. Timing the release to these moments can amplify reach.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<Checklist storageKey="ch5-l2-five-elements" title="The five elements — review checklist for the communication plan">
  <Item>**Early planning:** ToR includes communication; initial plan drafted at design phase; refined through field and reporting; finalised at dissemination.</Item>
  <Item>**Audience-focused:** stakeholder map mapped to audiences; each audience has a defined use of the results; channels match the audience's information environment.</Item>
  <Item>**Diversified products:** range of products produced; substance preserved across formats; products accessible long-term via multiple channels.</Item>
  <Item>**Minimum package:** every obligatory product produced; optional products selected for the context; simultaneous release.</Item>
  <Item>**Timely access:** release timed to the next programming cycle; alignment with national policy windows; integration with international/national advocacy days.</Item>
</Checklist>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Communication plan">The document — drafted at design, finalised at dissemination — that captures objectives, audiences, products, channels, timeline, and budget for the CPE's strategic communication.</Term>
  <Term name="Minimum communications package">The mandatory set of core products every CPE delivers — evaluation report, executive summary, brief, case studies, video, presentation.</Term>
  <Term name="Audience-centric approach">Determining the target audience, prioritising their information needs, packaging and delivering messages most relevantly to each.</Term>
  <Term name="Decision-making cycle">The internal and external moments (programming cycle preparation, national policy revisions, advocacy days) the team times the CPE release to reach.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
