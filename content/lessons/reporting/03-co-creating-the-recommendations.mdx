<Callout variant="key" title="Why co-creation">
The evaluation team is composed of independent external experts. They don't have full knowledge of all operational implications, nor of every ongoing or planned development in the country programme. To avoid one-size-fits-all suggestions and to produce **effective action-oriented proposals tailored to the evaluation users**, the CPE Manager involves the ERG in **co-creating** the recommendations.
</Callout>

Co-creation has three explicit purposes:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Adherence">Stakeholders who shaped the recommendations are more likely to back them.</KV>

  <KV title="Uptake">Recommendations co-designed with implementers are more likely to actually be implemented.</KV>

  <KV title="Practical fit">The ERG knows which actions are within the user's control and which aren't — recommendations must focus on actions within reach.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>Box 17 — The five-step co-creation process</h3>

<Checklist storageKey="ch4-l3-cocreation" title="Box 17 — Recommendations co-creation process">
  <Item>**Step 1.** Report v1 contains NO recommendations. Instead, the team formulates **tentative recommendations** in a dedicated **recommendations protocol and worksheet** — with rationale, priority, linked conclusion(s), target audience, and a short list of operational implications for each.</Item>
  <Item>**Step 2.** After QA, the CPE Manager shares the worksheet with ERG members along with the draft final report. ERG members review the tentative recommendations and provide input — propose edits, draft alternative wording, identify or modify operational implications, confirm or alter priority, amend target audience.</Item>
  <Item>**Step 3.** The CPE Manager together with the team leader consolidate ERG input and amend the set of recommendations accordingly.</Item>
  <Item>**Step 4.** The revised worksheet serves as basis for a **recommendations workshop**. This workshop takes place AFTER the ERG meeting where the report v1 was presented. Each revised recommendation — with its rationale and operational implications — is reviewed and refined to reach agreement on wording.</Item>
  <Item>**Step 5.** Final recommendations are inserted into the **CPE report v2**.</Item>
</Checklist>

<h3>What goes in the recommendations worksheet</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="The recommendation itself">A clear statement of the action to be taken.</KV>

  <KV title="Rationale">Why this recommendation — drawing on the findings and conclusions that motivated it.</KV>

  <KV title="Priority">High / medium / low — initially proposed by the team, possibly amended by the ERG.</KV>

  <KV title="Linked conclusion(s)">The specific conclusion(s) from which this recommendation stems.</KV>

  <KV title="Target audience">The business unit(s) charged with implementing the recommendation. May be amended by the ERG to add additional UNFPA business units that should be co-responsible.</KV>

  <KV title="Operational implications">A short list of what implementation would entail. Often expanded by ERG members who can foresee implications the team cannot.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>The reject rule</h3>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Recommendations must trace back to conclusions">
The CPE Manager ensures that when ERG members amend proposed recommendations — adding operational implications, suggesting new recommendations — these strictly derive from the **conclusions presented in the draft final report**. Any suggestion for a recommendation that **cannot be traced back to any of the CPE's conclusions must be rejected**. The chain from findings → conclusions → recommendations cannot be broken at the recommendations workshop.
</Callout>

<h3>Why co-creation works</h3>

Involving the CPE stakeholders in a structured co-creation process expands ownership and empowers evaluation users to develop solutions tailored to their circumstances. The iterative pattern looks like this:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Step A — tentative recommendations">Proposed by the evaluation team in the worksheet.</KV>

  <KV title="Step B — ERG reflection">Reflection and input by ERG members directly in the worksheet.</KV>

  <KV title="Step C — refinement">CPE Manager and team leader consolidate and refine.</KV>

  <KV title="Step D — group discussion">Recommendations workshop reviews, refines, and finalises each recommendation.</KV>

  <KV title="Step E — inclusion">Final recommendations inserted into the CPE report.</KV>
</KVGrid>

This sequence stimulates collective reflection to **incrementally specify the content** of each recommendation. The interaction between the team, the CPE Manager, and the ERG is key to ensuring **new and doable ideas** to improve the next country programme materialise during the CPE process.

<InPractice country="What “tested” means at the workshop">
At the workshop, each recommendation is tested for three things: **utility** (does it help users solve a real problem?), **feasibility** (can it actually be implemented given available resources and the country context?), and **conditions of success** (what has to be true for it to work?). Recommendations that fail any of these three tests are reworked or dropped.
</InPractice>

<Callout variant="tip" title="The political register">
The co-creation process also lets the team adjust the **political and interpersonal register** of recommendation wording — avoiding "red flag" words like *failure*, *lack*, *incompetence* and avoiding blame language, while still being clear about what needs to change. Where changes in the desired direction are already underway, the workshop is where this gets acknowledged — preventing the report from misleading readers or creating unnecessary backlash.
</Callout>

<h3>Common pitfalls in the recommendations chapter</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Untraced recommendation">The recommendation isn't supported by any conclusion — and therefore not by any findings.</KV>

  <KV title="Vague action">The recommendation is unclear about the actual operational implications.</KV>

  <KV title="No owner">The recommendation fails to specify who should take action.</KV>

  <KV title="Unrealistic">The recommendation isn't realistic regarding timing (priority level), resources, or political context.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Co-creation">The collaborative process by which evaluation team and ERG together shape the final wording, priority, target audience, and operational implications of each recommendation.</Term>
  <Term name="Recommendations protocol and worksheet">The structured template used through the five steps — first for the team's tentative recommendations, then for ERG input, then for the workshop.</Term>
  <Term name="Recommendations workshop">The structured stakeholder meeting (Step 4) where the consolidated set is reviewed against utility, feasibility, and conditions of success — and finalised.</Term>
  <Term name="Target audience">The UNFPA business unit(s) charged with implementing a given recommendation. Specified in the worksheet, sometimes expanded by the ERG.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
