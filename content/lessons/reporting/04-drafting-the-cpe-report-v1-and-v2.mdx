<Callout variant="key" title="What the iteration delivers">
Writing the CPE report is an iterative process during which the report is quality-assured, revised, refined and improved until it meets the rigour and quality standards in the ToR. The team leader reviews contributions from each member, flags weaknesses, and guides revision. The process is punctuated by **two in-depth quality-assurance stages** performed on **two versions** of the report.
</Callout>

## Version 1 of the report

The team writes the first version. It has the **same structure and contents** as the final version — with two exceptions:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="No executive summary in v1">The executive summary can only be drafted once the main body is in (or close to) its final stage. It appears in v2.</KV>

  <KV title="No recommendations chapter in v1">Tentative recommendations live in the **recommendations protocol and worksheet** — not yet in the report itself. The final recommendations are inserted into v2 only after the recommendations workshop (lesson 4.3).</KV>
</KVGrid>

The team leader checks v1 against the guidance on evaluation quality assurance and assessment (the EQA criteria). After that check, they send to the CPE Manager:

1. The CPE report (v1).
2. The evaluation matrix (with all supporting evidence).
3. The duly completed recommendations protocol and worksheet (with tentative recommendations).

### CPE Manager + RO M&E Adviser QA

The CPE Manager conducts in-depth quality control against the EQA grid criteria. They seek input from relevant CO staff to catch factual errors or misinterpretation of data. They also share the report with the **regional M&E Adviser** for review and guidance.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="If improvements are needed">The CPE Manager requests revision of the report and/or the worksheet — stating the specific changes needed and why.</KV>

  <KV title="If v1 passes">The CPE Manager shares the report AND the worksheet with the ERG, and sets dates for the ERG meeting and the recommendations workshop. Those two meetings should be **on two successive days**, with sufficient time allocated — at least half a day for the recommendations workshop.</KV>
</KVGrid>

## The ERG meeting and recommendations workshop

In anticipation, the CPE Manager circulates the draft report and the worksheet. The CPE Manager indicates **which sections** (findings and conclusions) need particularly close ERG input. ERG members get **2–3 weeks (10 to 15 working days)** to provide feedback. The CPE Manager may use a shared Google Drive file so all ERG comments appear in one document.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The CPE Manager as filter">
As the CPE Manager reviews ERG comments, they **filter out comments that are beyond the scope of the evaluation** — this keeps the team's work manageable as the deadline approaches. Filtering isn't censorship; it's editorial discipline to protect the analytical chain.
</Callout>

The CO Representative chairs the ERG meeting and moderates discussion. The CPE Manager tracks all comments — expressed during the meeting AND sent in writing — and compiles them for sharing with the team. The ERG meeting is the **last opportunity** for stakeholders to have the team double-check factual data, expand or detail analysis (findings), and justify judgement (conclusions).

### Recommendations workshop

The workshop reviews the consolidated set of recommendations (taking stock of ERG worksheet suggestions). The objective: **test the utility and feasibility** of each recommendation and finalise wording — including operational implications. The recommendations emerging from the workshop are the **final set** inserted in the final CPE report.

The CPE Manager (in consultation with the CO Representative and the government partner coordinating the country programme) may amend participant list — adding UNFPA personnel and partners who may be responsible for implementing some recommendation(s) and should therefore co-create.

## Version 2 of the report

The team takes stock of comments received and — **without compromising the independence of its value judgements** — revises into v2:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Methodological and factual">Comments on methodology and facts are systematically addressed. Where there are conflicting "facts", the team makes a judgement based on the provenance of data.</KV>

  <KV title="Substance">Comments on the substance of findings and conclusions are addressed by deepening the analysis and expanding findings — to the extent supported by available data.</KV>

  <KV title="Framing">Feedback on level of detail, language, and framing is used to sharpen findings and conclusions and make the report user-friendly.</KV>

  <KV title="Rejected comments">Some comments may be rejected by the team. The team leader explains the rationale for dissenting views in a note (the "audit trail") to the CPE Manager.</KV>

  <KV title="Recommendations inserted">The recommendations as finalised at the workshop are inserted into v2.</KV>

  <KV title="Executive summary written">Now that the main body is final, the executive summary is drafted.</KV>
</KVGrid>

### Final QA

The CPE Manager conducts in-depth quality control of v2 against the EQA grid criteria. They share with the RO M&E Adviser.

- **Where improvements are needed**, the CPE Manager requests revision with specific reasons.
- **When v2 passes**, the CPE Manager — in consultation with the RO M&E Adviser and the CO Representative — **formally approves** the report. Content is now considered final.

The CPE Manager shares the final report and the duly completed **EQA grid** with the RO M&E Adviser, who consolidates it based on regional feedback. The RO M&E Adviser then shares the final report with the **UNFPA IEO**, which performs a final quality assessment. The CPE report is published in the UNFPA evaluation database **together with the IEO's quality assessment**.

<InPractice country="The “audit trail” rule">
When the team rejects an ERG comment, they don't just ignore it. The team leader writes a note explaining the rationale for dissent, which goes to the CPE Manager. This audit trail is what protects the evaluators' independence while still keeping the process accountable to stakeholders.
</InPractice>

<Callout variant="key" title="Where independence sits">
The handbook is explicit: the team revises v2 **"without compromising the independence of its value judgements"**. ERG input shapes wording, sharpens framing, and corrects facts — but it does not override the evaluation team's reasoned conclusions. The audit trail makes the line visible.
</Callout>

## Communications plan update

As the team revises into v2, the CPE Manager and CO communication officer **revisit the initial communications plan** (developed at design phase). They adjust the timeline for evaluation products, the corresponding budget lines, and include new advocacy and communication opportunities surfaced during the ERG meeting and recommendations workshop. This sets up Phase 5 (Dissemination).

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="EQA grid">Evaluation Quality Assurance grid — the checklist used by the CPE Manager (and later the IEO) to assess the report against quality criteria.</Term>
  <Term name="Audit trail">The team leader's note explaining the rationale for any rejected ERG comment — protecting independence while keeping the process accountable.</Term>
  <Term name="Executive summary">A 5–6-page standalone summary written only for v2 — covering purpose, scope, methodology, main findings, conclusions, and recommendations.</Term>
  <Term name="IEO quality assessment">The Independent Evaluation Office's final assessment, published alongside the CPE report in the UNFPA evaluation database.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
