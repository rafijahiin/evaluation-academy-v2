<Callout variant="key" title="The reporting phase's central task">
A finding is a **detailed, evidence-based response to an evaluation question**. To draft the report, the team first sorts, arranges, complements (if still needed), processes, and weaves the data in the matrix into a meaningful account. Findings include **cause-and-effect statements** about UNFPA's contribution to observed changes — built using contribution analysis, not pure attribution.
</Callout>

<h3>Analytical techniques the team uses</h3>

Data analysis culminates in the reporting phase but starts during fieldwork. To process qualitative and quantitative data, the team applies techniques in combination:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Describing">Setting out what the data shows in concrete terms before interpreting it.</KV>

  <KV title="Counting">Quantifying where the data allows — frequency, proportion, trend.</KV>

  <KV title="Factoring">Identifying the variables that influence the observed results.</KV>

  <KV title="Clustering">Grouping similar data points to surface patterns.</KV>

  <KV title="Comparing">Within-programme and across stakeholders, sites, time periods.</KV>

  <KV title="Finding commonalities and differences">Looking for what is shared across cases AND what diverges.</KV>

  <KV title="Examining deviant cases / outliers">The case that doesn't fit often reveals the limit of the explanation.</KV>

  <KV title="Ruling out alternative explanations">Could the change be explained by external factors unrelated to UNFPA's interventions? If yes, the finding has to acknowledge that.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>Figure 6 — The contribution analysis flow</h3>

Findings come from a five-stage flow grounded in Mayne's contribution analysis:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="1. Identify the attribution problem">In the design phase, the EQs and assumptions for verification were refined to enable measurement of the extent UNFPA interventions brought about the observed results.</KV>

  <KV title="2. Critical analysis of the theory of change">Design-phase review of the country programme ToC.</KV>

  <KV title="3. Identify existing evidence of contribution">Through interviews and document review during the design phase.</KV>

  <KV title="4. Assess extent of contribution and gaps in evidence">Drawing on all data collection methods and triangulation across design and field phases.</KV>

  <KV title="5. Seek out additional evidence / refine assessment">Based on data analysis and interpretation across the field and reporting phases.</KV>
</KVGrid>

The output: **answers to evaluation questions** plus a **revisited theory of change** — both presented in the report.

<h3>Internal validity — interrogating every finding</h3>

As each team member works on the findings for the EQs they are responsible for (per the field-phase exit meeting), responses must be **evidence-based**. Not citing an opinion; not stating a personal point of view. The team systematically interrogates each finding:

<Checklist storageKey="ch4-l1-validity-check" title="Internal validity check — apply to every finding">
  <Item>Does it solidly rest on data that has been systematically triangulated (lesson 3.3)?</Item>
  <Item>Could the changes described be explained by external factors unrelated to UNFPA's interventions?</Item>
  <Item>Does it depart from (or contradict) findings of other evaluations — UNFPA centralized, thematic, programme evaluations; other UN agencies and INGOs; UNDAF/UNSDCF evaluations? If so, how is the divergence explained?</Item>
  <Item>Does it address the comments received during the debriefing meeting at the end of fieldwork?</Item>
</Checklist>

<h3>Quotation discipline</h3>

The team uses quotations only to **support the interpretations and explanations** in the findings — never to substitute for them. When used well, quotes:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Enrol the informant's voice">Adding vividness and depth that paraphrase cannot match.</KV>

  <KV title="Enhance readability">A pull quote breaks up dense analysis and signals importance.</KV>

  <KV title="Serve as evidence">Original data lets the reader assess the accuracy of the analysis. Confidence in the finding strengthens.</KV>

  <KV title="Maintain confidentiality">Quotes are attributed to a category of respondent ("rural clinic nurse", "young peer educator", "UN staff", "SGBV survivor") — never to a named individual.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Quotation pitfalls">
- Don't substitute a quotation for analysis — the team's reasoning has to be present in their own words.
- Don't quote opinions and call them findings — a finding is an evidence-based response, not an informant's view.
- Don't break confidentiality — identifying details must be removed even in the evaluation matrix annexed to the report.
</Callout>

<h3>Peer review and the matrix as the audit trail</h3>

Rigour comes from continuous discussion within the team and with the CPE Manager. Evaluators exchange write-ups and **act as peer reviewers for one another**. The reliability of findings is preserved by maintaining a meticulous record of supporting data in the matrix — so the reader can, at any time, trace any finding to the evidence that produced it.

<InPractice country="Common pitfalls (per section 4.5 guidance on Chapter 4)">
The handbook calls out four pitfalls in the findings chapter:

1. Findings are not well organised and don't address the EQ — the reader has to figure out where they fit.
2. Findings lack precision or context — the reader can't interpret their relative strength.
3. Findings only partly respond to the EQ — only some assumptions appear tested.
4. Findings express judgements and get mixed with conclusions.
</InPractice>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Finding">An evidence-based, triangulated response to an evaluation question — a detailed answer, not a summary or opinion.</Term>
  <Term name="Contribution statement">A finding that explains how UNFPA's interventions plausibly contributed to an observed change, alongside other factors.</Term>
  <Term name="Internal validity">The strength of the chain between data, evidence, and finding — verified by the 4-point interrogation.</Term>
  <Term name="Audit trail">The evaluation matrix annexed to the report, letting the reader trace any finding to its specific underlying data.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
