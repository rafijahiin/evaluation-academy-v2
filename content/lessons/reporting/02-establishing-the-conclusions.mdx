<Callout variant="key" title="A conclusion is a synthesis with a value judgement">
A conclusion is **not a summary** or re-statement of findings — it is a **synthesis of key points, topics or themes**: common patterns across programmatic areas; recurrent causes of weaknesses; or specific success or failure factors. Unlike findings, conclusions involve a **value judgement** by the evaluators on the merit and performance of the country programme. They must, however, be free from personal bias and supported entirely by the findings.
</Callout>

<h3>Where conclusions come from</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="From the findings">Each conclusion synthesises a number of findings — never a single finding alone. The chain must be traceable.</KV>

  <KV title="From crosscutting issues">Conclusions also emerge from transversal themes the team noticed across data analysis — patterns that don't belong to one EQ but cut across several.</KV>

  <KV title="With value judgement, not opinion">The team's judgement on merit / performance is what makes it a conclusion. But the judgement must be supported by evidence, not personal preference.</KV>

  <KV title="Without action statements">Conclusions don't entail action. They lead to recommendations — but they themselves don't recommend.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>What makes a good conclusion</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Limited in number">The CPE Manager ensures the team formulates a **limited** set of conclusions — not a long list. Section 4.5 sets a maximum of 5 pages total for the conclusions chapter.</KV>

  <KV title="Concise and to the point">Conclusions should not offer unnecessary detail about the findings, nor comment on methodology. They are end-result statements, not commentary.</KV>

  <KV title="Free of preconceived notions">The CPE Manager must ensure conclusions don't derive from opinions formed outside the evaluation — particularly by team leaders who may have conducted CPEs in other countries and may be tempted to reuse prior writing.</KV>

  <KV title="Balanced">The team leader and CPE Manager verify conclusions are NOT systematically skewed toward positive views. Weaknesses, bottlenecks, drawbacks and challenges must not be ignored. A whitewashed CPE has no value to programme decision-makers.</KV>

  <KV title="Origin clearly indicated">Per section 4.5, each conclusion in the report must specify **(i) Origin** — response to evaluation questions X, Y; **(ii) Associated recommendations** — which subsequent recommendations link back.</KV>

  <KV title="Categorised where useful">Conclusions can be organised into categories — e.g. **strategic conclusions** (UNFPA's ability to achieve its goals and objectives) followed by **programmatic conclusions** (specific thematic areas).</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>Common pitfalls in the conclusions chapter</h3>

<Callout variant="warn" title="What the handbook tells the CPE Manager to catch">
Per section 4.5 guidance on the conclusions chapter, common pitfalls include conclusions that:

1. **Restate findings** — just repeating with different words.
2. **Amount to vague statements** — non-specific, non-actionable, non-judgemental observations.
3. **Are based on one finding only** — when synthesis requires multiple findings.
4. **Contain recommendations or action points** — they should set up the recommendations chapter, not pre-empt it.
5. **Are too long and offer unnecessary detail** — analytical prose belongs in the findings chapter.
</Callout>

<InPractice country="The transparency test">
The team must be able to establish the reasoning by which a conclusion was reached. A reader should be able to ask "which findings led you here, and what value judgement did you make?" — and get a clear answer. If the team can't reconstruct the reasoning, the conclusion isn't ready for the report.
</InPractice>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Conclusion">A synthesis of multiple findings carrying the evaluators' value judgement on the merit or performance of the country programme.</Term>
  <Term name="Value judgement">The evaluators' assessment of strengths, weaknesses, successes, failures — supported by findings, free from personal bias.</Term>
  <Term name="Strategic conclusion">A conclusion focused on UNFPA's overall ability to achieve its goals and objectives in the country.</Term>
  <Term name="Programmatic conclusion">A conclusion focused on a specific thematic area — SRHR, GEWE, adolescents and youth, population dynamics.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
