<Callout variant="key" title="The structure tells the reader where things live">
To be useful, the CPE report must provide evidence-based responses to the evaluation questions, leading to justifiable conclusions, leading to actionable recommendations (Box 16). It reflects sound data collection and analysis (with their limitations) and is structured so readers can **find the information they need quickly**. The structure also lets evaluators break a relatively long document into manageable sections, each drafted by different team members.
</Callout>

The total report length — excluding opening pages and annexes — is capped at **80 pages**.

## The cover and opening pages

The CO communication officer develops the cover using the UNFPA template (Adobe Illustrator + Microsoft Word) — with prescribed fonts and styles for consistent branding across CPEs.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Country map">Showing UNFPA programme implementation areas — 1 page.</KV>

  <KV title="CPE team and manager table">Names, titles, positions in the team — 1 page.</KV>

  <KV title="Copyright and disclaimer">Standard UNFPA copyright; disclaimer that analysis and recommendations do not necessarily reflect the views of UNFPA.</KV>

  <KV title="ERG members table">Names, titles/positions, organisations.</KV>

  <KV title="Acknowledgements">1 page.</KV>

  <KV title="Table of contents">With page numbering — 2 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="Tables and figures">Numbers, titles, page references — 2 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="Acronyms and abbreviations">Only those used in the report — 2 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="Key facts table">Geography, government, economy, demographics, youth, fertility, HIV, harmful practices, SDGs — 2 pages.</KV>
</KVGrid>

Opening pages total **10–13 pages**, numbered in Roman numerals.

## Executive summary

Written **only for v2**, once the main body is final or near-final.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Purpose and scope, intended audience">Half a page.</KV>

  <KV title="Methodology">Half a page.</KV>

  <KV title="Main findings">2 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="Conclusions">1.5 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="Recommendations">1.5 pages.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<Callout variant="tip" title="Why the executive summary matters disproportionately">
Because of its conciseness, the executive summary **can be more influential and read by more readers than the main body** of the report. The team leader and CPE Manager pay close attention to drafting it. It should be understandable as a stand-alone document — disseminated separately from the full report, enabling senior management to grasp the evaluation's results without reading 80 pages.
</Callout>

Maximum length: **5–6 pages**. Arabic numerals begin here and run to the end of the report (including annexes).

## Chapter 1: Introduction (9–11 pages)

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="1.1 Purpose and objectives of the CPE">Half a page.</KV>

  <KV title="1.2 Scope">Thematic (EQs with assumptions for verification), geographic, and temporal — 2 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="1.3 Evaluation approach">1 page overview.</KV>

  <KV title="1.3.1 Contribution analysis and theory of change">3 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="1.3.2 Methods for data collection and analysis">3 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="1.3.3 Stakeholders consulted and sites visited">2 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="1.3.4 Limitations and mitigation measures">1–2 pages — the team reports factors that restricted access to information and may have affected data quantity/quality. Where limitations couldn't be mitigated, they explain the extent to which validity and reliability are affected.</KV>
</KVGrid>

Much of Chapter 1's information is already in the design report; it gets updated, refined (e.g. sampling approach), expanded, or shortened.

## Chapter 2: Country context (5–6 pages)

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="2.1 Development challenges and national strategies">5 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="2.2 The role of external assistance">1 page.</KV>
</KVGrid>

Essentially the design report's Chapter 2, updated and refined.

## Chapter 3: United Nations and UNFPA response (7–10 pages)

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="3.1 UN and UNFPA strategic response">1 page.</KV>

  <KV title="3.2 UNFPA response through the country programme">1 page.</KV>

  <KV title="3.2.1 Brief description of UNFPA previous programme cycle">Goals and achievements — 1 page.</KV>

  <KV title="3.2.2 The current UNFPA country programme and ToC analysis">3 pages. Presents the theory of change as reconstructed during the design phase AND refined during data analysis and reporting. The evaluators examine clarity, validity and comprehensiveness of pathways of change; identify shortcomings and their consequences; highlight where pathways differed from initial projections; identify where key assumptions about external factors were overlooked or insufficiently addressed.</KV>

  <KV title="3.2.3 Financial structure of the UNFPA country programme">3 pages.</KV>
</KVGrid>

## Chapter 4: Findings (25–35 pages)

The bulk of the report. Each section corresponds to one evaluation question:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Section 4.1">Answer to EQ 1 — 4 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="Section 4.2">Answer to EQ 2 — 4 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="Section 4.3">Answer to EQ 3 — 4 pages.</KV>

  <KV title="Section 4.4 onwards">One section per remaining EQ — 4 pages each.</KV>
</KVGrid>

Each section opens with a summary box. Underneath the summary, evaluators add: *"For details of the evidence supporting findings in section 4.X, see Evaluation matrix : Assumptions X, Y, Z... (in annex 1)."*

## Chapter 5: Conclusions (max 5 pages)

<Callout variant="key" title="The relationship reader needs to see">
While the report structure keeps findings and conclusions separate, evaluators must ensure readers can see the **clear and logical relationship** between them. Conclusions are presented in order of importance; they can be organised in categories (e.g. strategic conclusions followed by programmatic conclusions). For each conclusion, the report indicates:

- **(i) Origin:** Response to evaluation questions X, Y...
- **(ii) Associated recommendation(s):** X, Y...
</Callout>

Half a page per conclusion. The conclusion is the end-result of a process of analysis and reasoning grounded in evidence-based findings — not a summary, not a re-statement.

## Chapter 6: Recommendations (max 6 pages)

**Not included in v1.** In v1, tentative recommendations live in the worksheet. The content of Chapter 6 in v2 results directly from the recommendations as finalised during the recommendations workshop.

Recommendations are presented in order of importance. For each, the report indicates:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="(i) Type">Strategic (UNFPA's ability to achieve goals) vs. programmatic or operational.</KV>

  <KV title="(ii) Priority">High / medium / low.</KV>

  <KV title="(iii) Based on conclusions">Specific conclusions from which the recommendation stems.</KV>

  <KV title="(iv) Directed to">Target audience — the business units charged with implementation.</KV>

  <KV title="(v) Operational implications">A short list of what implementation would entail.</KV>
</KVGrid>

Note: the **timeframe for implementation** is provided in the management response (lesson 5.3), not in the report itself.

## Annexes

All five annexes are mandatory. The ToR always comes as the final annex.

<Checklist storageKey="ch4-l5-annexes" title="Mandatory annexes to the CPE report">
  <Item>Annex 1: Evaluation matrix.</Item>
  <Item>Annex 2: List of documents consulted.</Item>
  <Item>Annex 3: List of persons met and their organisational affiliations / institutions.</Item>
  <Item>Annex 4: Data collection tools.</Item>
  <Item>Annex 5: CPE Terms of Reference.</Item>
</Checklist>

<InPractice country="The pages add up">
Total: ~10–13 (opening) + 5–6 (executive summary) + 9–11 (Ch 1) + 5–6 (Ch 2) + 7–10 (Ch 3) + 25–35 (Ch 4) + 5 (Ch 5) + 6 (Ch 6) = roughly 72–92 pages of main body + opening. The 80-page cap (excluding opening + annexes) is tight by design — it forces evaluators to write tight, edit aggressively, and prioritise the most important findings and conclusions.
</InPractice>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Annex 1">The evaluation matrix — the system of record for every finding's underlying data.</Term>
  <Term name="Annex 5">The Terms of Reference — always the final annex of the report.</Term>
  <Term name="Strategic vs programmatic recommendation">Strategic recommendations address UNFPA's overall goal achievement; programmatic recommendations address specific thematic areas; operational recommendations address implementation mechanics.</Term>
  <Term name="Summary box">The 4–6 sentence boxed paragraph that opens each EQ-response section in Chapter 4 — gives the reader a takeaway before the detail.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
