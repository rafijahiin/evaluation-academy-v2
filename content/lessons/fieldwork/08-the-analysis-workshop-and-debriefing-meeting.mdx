<Callout variant="key" title="What sections 3.3 + 3.4 do together">
**Section 3.3** governs how the evaluation matrix is used as the team's working database during the field phase — what goes in, when, and how triangulation works in practice. **Section 3.4** covers the structured close of the field phase: an internal analysis workshop, a debriefing with the ERG and CO, and the follow-up that lets the team enter the reporting phase with a clean handover.
</Callout>

## Part 1 — The evaluation matrix during fieldwork

At the end of the design phase, the CPE Manager places the matrix in a shared drive folder accessible to every team member at all times. Throughout the field phase, the **team leader monitors data entry by each team member** — ensuring data is recorded regularly, well organised, triangulated, and that gaps are identified and addressed.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Connectivity workaround">Where in-country connectivity is patchy, team members work in an offline copy of the matrix and periodically synchronise with the online version.</KV>

  <KV title="Regular meetings">The team leader calls regular team meetings to start analysing and interpreting compiled data — surfacing recurring issues and emerging themes, which may lead to adjustments to the data collection work plan (new sources, modified interview guides, follow-up questionnaires).</KV>

  <KV title="The matrix as the system of record">All data — qualitative and quantitative; primary and secondary — lives in the matrix. Entries include direct quotes and excerpts from documents, observation notes from sites, and may include photos and links.</KV>

  <KV title="Quote attribution rules">In both the matrix and the eventual report, quotes are attributed to a **category of respondent** ("rural clinic nurse", "UN staff", "young peer educator", "SGBV survivor") — never to a named individual. Anonymity is non-negotiable.</KV>

  <KV title="Cross-referencing data">Where the same data is relevant to verify multiple assumptions across different EQs, the team **records (repeats) it under each relevant evaluation question** in the matrix. Don't make readers chase cross-references.</KV>
</KVGrid>

## Part 2 — Triangulation in practice

Triangulation is the combination of multiple methods and sources in assessing the same issue — for the purpose of increasing the credibility of findings. **At a minimum**, the team uses **two different sources** to corroborate a piece of information. Examples: one authoritative document plus one interview; two separate interviews from different stakeholder categories.

<Callout variant="warn" title="What happens when triangulation is weak">
Data obtained via one method only, and not corroborated by other sources, **should not be used to validate evaluation results**. Where triangulation isn't sufficiently strong, the corresponding findings in the CPE report must be presented as **"tentative findings"** — with the biases arising from too few methods or insufficiently solid sources explicitly noted.
</Callout>

## Part 3 — The analysis workshop

At the end of the field phase, upon return to the capital city, the CPE Manager gathers the team in an **analysis workshop** to review the data collected and assess its validity and reliability. With the CPE Manager, the team reviews how far the information in the matrix verifies the assumptions corresponding to each evaluation question.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Step 1 — Consolidate the matrix">Tidy entries; resolve formatting; ensure every cell has a clear source.</KV>

  <KV title="Step 2 — Identify gaps">Where the matrix is thin — assumptions with little data, or contradictions between equally reliable sources — flag what still needs more data.</KV>

  <KV title="Step 3 — Highlight limitations">Where contradictions persist between strong sources, explicitly note them and the implications for use.</KV>

  <KV title="Step 4 — Preliminary findings">Per evaluation question, identify the preliminary findings to be presented at the debriefing meeting.</KV>

  <KV title="Step 5 — Theory of change check">Based on preliminary findings, assess how the country programme theory of change works in practice; identify its limitations; reflect on how it might be strengthened.</KV>

  <KV title="Step 6 — Unintended consequences">Identify possible unintended consequences — positive and negative — arising from outputs and their contribution to outcomes.</KV>
</KVGrid>

### Table 11 — Threats to validity of findings

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Field phase — filtering data">Risks: information has low/no logical connection to indicators, so it can't inform them; data are of low quality (contradictory, biased toward one stakeholder category). Action: filter out unreliable or off-chain data before formulating preliminary findings.</KV>

  <KV title="Field + Reporting — formulating findings">Risks: evaluators don't discuss and resolve contradictions from data collected; evaluators don't have evidence to explain how UNFPA plausibly contributed to observed changes. Action: revisit triangulation; pursue follow-up data where the chain of evidence is broken.</KV>

  <KV title="Reporting — answering EQs">Risks: answers to EQs aren't evidence-based (don't logically build on data); data hasn't been analysed and interpreted enough to provide meaningful information. Action: reorganise the analysis around the matrix; tighten the chain from data → evidence → finding → conclusion.</KV>
</KVGrid>

## Part 4 — The debriefing meeting

Following the analysis workshop, the CPE Manager organises a **debriefing meeting** with the ERG and relevant CO staff. The meeting is an opportunity for the team to present preliminary findings orally, obtain feedback, and verify information — while also strengthening ownership of the evaluation by the CO.

<Callout variant="key" title="The boundary at debriefing">
At the debriefing — both in the presentation and during discussion — the evaluators **refrain from formulating any conclusion (judgement) and from suggesting any recommendations**. The boundary protects the analytical sequence: findings → conclusions → recommendations comes later, after the analysis workshop's preliminary findings have been tested against feedback and any follow-up data.
</Callout>

The CPE Manager facilitates a Q&A session after the presentation. Participants are invited to seek clarification or provide additional information. The CPE Manager invites participants to **test the evaluators' interpretations and extrapolations** — checking whether they're justified and supported by sound arguments. ERG members and CO staff may flag gaps, provide complementary information, indicate further data sources, or rectify what they consider inaccurate interpretation.

## Part 5 — Follow-up and the exit meeting

After the debriefing, the CPE Manager writes meeting minutes, compiles ERG and CO feedback, and shares it with the team. They follow up to obtain — from ERG members or CO staff — documents supporting their statements (especially where these contradict or help refine the data already collected). They facilitate access to new key informants and assist the team in organising follow-up meetings, interviews, and additional documentation.

At the end of the field phase, the CPE Manager calls an **exit meeting** with the team. Together with the team leader, they review the CPE roadmap and agree:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Next steps">Delivery of the draft final report; quality assurance by the CPE Manager; presentation to the ERG; recommendations workshop; etc.</KV>

  <KV title="Deadlines">Agreed for each next-step deliverable.</KV>

  <KV title="Report structure">Reviewed against section 4.5; any pending questions clarified.</KV>

  <KV title="Task distribution">Team leader indicates who in the team is responsible for which section of the report, and the deadlines for each team member's contribution.</KV>

  <KV title="EQA grid review">The Evaluation Quality Assurance grid the CPE Manager will use to assess the report.</KV>

  <KV title="Communications plan update">The CPE Manager and CO communication officer revisit the initial communications plan — adding audiences or advocacy opportunities surfaced during fieldwork.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<InPractice country="The handover that makes reporting possible">
The exit meeting is what lets the reporting phase begin cleanly. Without it, the team enters report-writing with unresolved gaps, undocumented changes to the matrix, and unclear ownership of sections. The CPE Manager treats the meeting as a structured handover — the difference between a report delivered on time and a report that drags through endless revisions.
</InPractice>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Triangulation">Combining multiple methods AND sources to assess the same issue — the minimum standard is two different sources corroborating one finding.</Term>
  <Term name="Tentative finding">A finding in the CPE report where triangulation is insufficient — explicitly labelled so users know the strength of evidence behind it.</Term>
  <Term name="Analysis workshop">The team's internal session at the end of the field phase to consolidate the matrix, identify gaps, and surface preliminary findings.</Term>
  <Term name="Debriefing meeting">The structured presentation of preliminary findings to the ERG and CO — Q&A only, no conclusions, no recommendations.</Term>
  <Term name="Exit meeting">CPE Manager + team leader handover at the close of the field phase — next steps, deadlines, report structure, task distribution.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
