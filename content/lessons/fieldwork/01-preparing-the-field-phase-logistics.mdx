<Callout variant="key" title="One person owns this">
The CPE Manager is responsible for ensuring **all logistical and practical arrangements** for data collection are in place before the field phase begins. This includes transport, accommodation, security, government authorisations, interpreters, refreshments, travel allowances, and venue booking — and chasing each item to confirmation.
</Callout>

<h3>What the CPE Manager arranges</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Transport and travel authorisation">Hired vehicles or UNFPA cars to all sites; required travel authorisations issued for evaluators and interpreters; travel expense claim process explained ahead of time.</KV>

  <KV title="Accommodation">Booked in advance for every overnight site; consultants advised to retain all receipts for reimbursement.</KV>

  <KV title="Security briefing">A formal security briefing in line with UNDSS requirements before the team enters the field.</KV>

  <KV title="Government authorisations and permits">National and regional permissions verified well in advance; courtesy visits arranged where protocol requires it.</KV>

  <KV title="Refreshments and stipends">Refreshments planned for group interviews; in remote areas, small stipends for participants whose travel to the meeting site costs them income.</KV>

  <KV title="Interview venues">Suitable alternatives if a stakeholder's office is unsuitable; private space for group interviews and focus groups; accessible venues for participants with disabilities.</KV>

  <KV title="Interpreters">Selected for fluency in local languages AND familiarity with UNFPA's mandate (SRHR, GEWE, population dynamics, maternal health); gender-matched where culturally required; sign-language interpreters arranged where needed.</KV>

  <KV title="Communications backup">Phone or SIM card for evaluators in the field — so the CPE Manager and team leader stay in touch when internet drops out, meetings reschedule, or emergencies arise.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>The no-UNFPA-staff-at-data-collection rule</h3>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Bias prevention">
**No UNFPA staff** — including field-based staff and drivers — are allowed to participate in or attend any of the data collection activities. The reason is bias prevention: informants change what they say when they think UNFPA is in the room. The CPE Manager themselves may participate in *some* data collection only if their presence demonstrably cannot affect outcomes — typically in focus groups where rights-holders are unaware of the supporting agency.
</Callout>

A CO staff member, government delegate, or implementing partner may **accompany** the evaluators to introduce them at a site — but does not stay for the interview.

<h3>Ethical preparation for sensitive interviews</h3>

For interviews with **people with disabilities** and **SGBV survivors**, the CPE Manager arranges a gender-sensitive approach, accessible venues, and high-privacy spaces. SGBV is a particularly sensitive area; the team must follow WHO ethical and safety guidelines, with a lack of attention potentially putting both respondents and evaluators at risk.

In humanitarian contexts, issues must be addressed using existing and contextualised guidelines. The CPE Manager also ensures the CO communication officer briefs the team on **ethical photography** during data collection.

<h3>Box 10 — CPE Manager's checklist for field-work preparation</h3>

<Checklist storageKey="ch3-l1-fieldwork-prep" title="Box 10 — Field-work preparation checklist">
  <Item>Travel authorisations (if required) are issued.</Item>
  <Item>Ethical clearance from relevant national research body (if required) has been requested and obtained.</Item>
  <Item>Security briefing has taken place.</Item>
  <Item>Transportation of consultants to sites is organised (hired vehicle or UNFPA car).</Item>
  <Item>Communications with evaluators while in the field are arranged (phone or SIM card for continuity).</Item>
  <Item>Accommodations are booked.</Item>
  <Item>Consultants have been informed to keep all receipts for reimbursement.</Item>
  <Item>CO Representative's letter announcing the CPE to all stakeholders has been sent.</Item>
  <Item>Letters of request for interviews/meetings drafted and sent; receipts acknowledged.</Item>
  <Item>Follow-up done to ensure all key informants have confirmed availability.</Item>
  <Item>Interpreters contracted; transport and accommodation arranged; briefed on themes. Sign-language interpreters if needed.</Item>
  <Item>Participants in group interviews/focus groups have confirmed; coordinated by UNFPA staff or implementing partner on site.</Item>
  <Item>Adequate venues organised for each meeting (privacy, accessibility for persons with disabilities).</Item>
  <Item>Refreshments planned.</Item>
  <Item>Arrangements for defraying group-interview participants' travel costs.</Item>
  <Item>Consent and assent forms (prepared by team leader) printed in sufficient copies.</Item>
  <Item>Photography consent forms prepared by CO communication officer.</Item>
  <Item>CO staff member (or government delegate / IP representative) confirmed to accompany evaluators, make introductions, and NOT participate in meetings.</Item>
</Checklist>

<InPractice country="A back-up plan, always">
The handbook is explicit that the CPE Manager and team leader must have a back-up plan. If the security situation changes after the field-phase agenda is set, the team needs to review the work plan — switching to virtual interviews, selecting other sites, or rescheduling. The discipline of preparing for disruption is what makes the field phase robust.
</InPractice>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="UNDSS">United Nations Department of Safety and Security — the body whose security requirements govern UN fieldwork. Briefings are mandatory.</Term>
  <Term name="DSA">Daily Subsistence Allowance — UN reimbursement rate covering accommodation and meals during fieldwork.</Term>
  <Term name="Assent form">Used for participants not legally able to give full informed consent (e.g. minors) — typically alongside a parental/guardian consent form.</Term>
  <Term name="Ethical clearance">Approval from a national research ethics body, required in some countries before data collection on human subjects can proceed.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
