<Callout variant="key" title="Why the map matters">
The CPE Manager and the evaluators need a **comprehensive overview of the country programme stakeholders**. The map includes people who have been involved in both the preparation and implementation of the country programme, as well as those who have been directly or indirectly affected by its implementation.
</Callout>

The **R2U ToR Annex B** provides two tables that the CPE Manager (with the contribution of relevant CO staff) must complete, annex to the final ToR, and make available to the evaluators at the start of their assignment.

<StakeholderQuadrant />

<h3>The categories of stakeholders</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Government partners">Lead ministries coordinating the country programme; line ministries (health, education, planning, statistics, gender); subnational and decentralised government structures.</KV>

  <KV title="Non-governmental partners">National and international NGOs; civil society organisations; faith-based organisations.</KV>

  <KV title="Women-led and youth-led organisations">Specifically named in the handbook as key categories — given UNFPA's mandate and the LNOB principle.</KV>

  <KV title="Donor partners">Bilateral and multilateral donors funding UNFPA country programme work — including humanitarian donors where applicable.</KV>

  <KV title="UN agencies">Sister UN agencies operating in the country, especially where joint programmes intersect with the CPD.</KV>

  <KV title="Academia and research">Universities, research institutes, and think tanks with expertise relevant to SRHR, GEWE, adolescents and youth, or population dynamics.</KV>

  <KV title="Rights-holders">Representatives of different population groups among the UNFPA country programme intended beneficiaries — including the most vulnerable. Women, adolescents, youth, people with disabilities, SGBV survivors, key populations (LGBTQIA+, sex workers), and migrants / displaced populations.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>How evaluators use the map</h3>

The stakeholders **serve as essential sources of information** for the evaluators. During the field phase, the evaluators collect quantitative and qualitative data from them for the analysis of UNFPA support and contribution to each outcome of the country programme.

Critically, the evaluators will use the stakeholder map as the **basis for stakeholder sampling during the design phase** (lesson 2.3). The mapping is not just a list — it informs which voices the CPE will hear, and (just as importantly) which it will not.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The map is annexed to the final ToR">
The stakeholder map is **annexed to the final ToR** and shared with the selected evaluation team — not with the candidates during the call for consultancy. This is part of the two-stage ToR drafting process (lesson 1.4).
</Callout>

<h3>Building the map well: practical guidance</h3>

<Checklist storageKey="ch1-l9-stakeholder-map" title="Stakeholder map quality checklist">
  <Item>Every category from R2U ToR Annex B is covered — no category left empty.</Item>
  <Item>Stakeholders associated with seemingly well-performing and poorly performing interventions are both included (see lesson 2.3, Table 7 criterion 2).</Item>
  <Item>Both ongoing and completed interventions across the evaluation period are represented (Table 7 criterion 3).</Item>
  <Item>Stakeholders from the capital city and from other regions / provinces / districts (Table 7 criterion 4).</Item>
  <Item>Stakeholders associated with both financially large and financially modest AWPs (Table 7 criterion 5).</Item>
  <Item>Stakeholders working in the same field as UNFPA but who are NOT UNFPA partners (Table 7 criterion 8) — these are essential for triangulation.</Item>
  <Item>Rights-holder representatives — including those most vulnerable and furthest left behind.</Item>
  <Item>In countries with a humanitarian component: humanitarian cluster/sector leads and members (Table 7 criterion 1).</Item>
  <Item>Contact information current, with role / position and connection to specific CPD outputs.</Item>
</Checklist>

<InPractice country="From the handbook">
The handbook is explicit that the map must include "people who have been involved in both the preparation and the implementation of the country programme, as well as those who have been directly or indirectly affected by its implementation." Affected populations are stakeholders. A map that lists only delivery partners is incomplete.
</InPractice>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="R2U ToR Annex B">The template containing two tables that capture the country programme's stakeholder universe. Completed by the CPE Manager with CO staff input.</Term>
  <Term name="Rights-holders">In UNFPA evaluation language, the people and population groups intended to benefit from the country programme. Always treated as stakeholders, not just as data subjects.</Term>
  <Term name="LNOB">"Leave No One Behind" — the 2030 Agenda principle. The handbook expects rights-holders from marginalised groups to be visible in the stakeholder map by name (category).</Term>
  <Term name="Sampling basis">Lesson 2.3 — the evaluators will use the stakeholder map (plus their documentary review and first-round interviews) to draw a purposive, illustrative sample for field interviews.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
