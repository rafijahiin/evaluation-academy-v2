<Callout variant="key" title="What the catalogue is for">
The CO annual reports, annual results plans, and Annual Work Plans (AWPs) form the basis for consultants to gain a **comprehensive overview of the activities planned and undertaken** by the CO and implementing partners to address each output and outcome during the period of evaluation. The catalogue consolidates the financial and operational picture into a single spreadsheet.
</Callout>

<h3>What goes in</h3>

With the support of CO staff and the YEE, the CPE Manager must compile (**in Excel format**) a list of:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="All intervention budgets">Every intervention's planned budget for the period under evaluation — by year.</KV>

  <KV title="All expenditures">Actual expenditures by year, to compare against budgets and reveal where execution diverged from planning.</KV>

  <KV title="Annual data">Disaggregated by year of the country programme cycle.</KV>

  <KV title="Core / non-core split">Where applicable, breakdown by core vs non-core funding source.</KV>

  <KV title="Implementing partner">The implementing partner or agency executing each intervention.</KV>

  <KV title="Thematic and outcome links">Each intervention's link to outputs and contribution to outcomes in the country programme's results framework.</KV>
</KVGrid>

The template is available in **R2U ToR Annex C**, with a guidance note on *Establishing the list of UNFPA interventions* in the CPE Management Kit.

<h3>What the evaluators do with it</h3>

The catalogue serves three purposes for the evaluation team:

1. **Understanding of programme scope.** A clear understanding of all that was planned and/or implemented (including revisions to the original country programme) during the programme cycle.
2. **Stakeholder identification.** The list of interventions helps evaluators identify key partners and stakeholders that should be consulted as part of the evaluation process — feeding into the stakeholder map (lesson 1.9) and the sampling work in the design phase (lesson 2.3).
3. **Financial trend analysis.** The annual financial data on intervention budgets and expenditures lets evaluators see where the country programme accelerated, contracted, or pivoted across the cycle.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Include the joint work">
**UN joint programmes and joint work plans and reports are also to be included** for the evaluators' review. The catalogue is not just UNFPA-only interventions — wherever UNFPA has contributed to a joint programme, that intervention belongs in the spreadsheet.
</Callout>

<InPractice country="From the handbook">
The CO might have implemented 60–80 distinct interventions across a four-year programme cycle. A well-maintained catalogue lets the evaluation team scope the relative weight of each thematic area in minutes — and lets them prioritise which interventions to interrogate in depth versus which to cover via document review only.
</InPractice>

<Checklist storageKey="ch1-l8-catalogue-readiness" title="Catalogue readiness checklist">
  <Item>R2U ToR Annex C template downloaded and adapted for the country programme.</Item>
  <Item>Every UNFPA intervention for the period under evaluation listed with a unique identifier.</Item>
  <Item>Budgets entered by year for each intervention.</Item>
  <Item>Expenditures entered by year for each intervention.</Item>
  <Item>Implementing partner identified for each intervention.</Item>
  <Item>UN joint programmes and joint work plans included.</Item>
  <Item>Each intervention linked to outputs / outcomes in the country programme results framework.</Item>
  <Item>Spreadsheet placed in the document repository in the agreed folder.</Item>
  <Item>Shared with the evaluation team at the start of the design phase.</Item>
</Checklist>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="R2U ToR Annex C">The Excel template provided by the IEO for listing all UNFPA interventions, budgets, and expenditures for the period under evaluation.</Term>
  <Term name="Intervention">A discrete, planned UNFPA-supported activity or set of activities with a defined budget, implementing partner, and contribution to an output or outcome.</Term>
  <Term name="Core vs non-core funding">Core = unrestricted contributions to UNFPA. Non-core = earmarked / restricted funds from donors. The mix often shapes which interventions get implemented.</Term>
  <Term name="Joint programme">Programme jointly designed and / or implemented by multiple UN agencies — UNFPA's contribution still needs to appear in the catalogue.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
