<Callout variant="key" title="What the ERG does">
The ERG is formed by key internal and external evaluation stakeholders who act as **technical advisers throughout the evaluation process**. They review and provide comments on the ToR, the design report, and the CPE report. They participate in the co-creation of the CPE recommendations and support dissemination. As representatives of key stakeholders, selected ERG members are also interviewed by the evaluation team.
</Callout>

<h3>Membership: a balanced internal/external mix</h3>

The ERG is composed of both internal and external experts and stakeholders, reflecting a balanced representation of those with whom the CO has engaged in designing and implementing the country programme.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Internal — within UNFPA">Deputy / head of programmes or Assistant Representative; lead technical CO staff from all outcome areas; the operations manager; the CPE Manager; the RO M&E Adviser; and select RO technical advisers.</KV>

  <KV title="Government partner">Lead ministry coordinating the country programme; other relevant government institutions.</KV>

  <KV title="Implementing partners">Organisations that have implemented UNFPA-supported interventions during the period under evaluation.</KV>

  <KV title="UN system">United Nations agencies operating in the country, especially where joint programmes intersect with the CPD.</KV>

  <KV title="Donor and development partners">Funders and partner agencies relevant to UNFPA thematic areas. In humanitarian contexts, humanitarian donors and partners.</KV>

  <KV title="Civil society and rights-holders">NGOs, CSOs, women- and youth-led organisations, and representatives of rights-holders organisations — with specific attention to marginalised groups and young people.</KV>

  <KV title="Academia / methodology experts">External experts in thematic areas relevant to the country programme, or in evaluation methodology.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>The four moments when the ERG is consulted</h3>

<Checklist storageKey="ch1-l5-erg-moments" title="ERG consultation cadence">
  <Item>**Review of the draft Terms of Reference** — comments on the preliminary evaluation questions, methodology and scope (lesson 1.4).</Item>
  <Item>**Review of the Design Report version 1** — comments on the evaluators' critical analysis of the theory of change, the evaluation matrix, country context, and UN/UNFPA response (lesson 2.4.2).</Item>
  <Item>**Review of the CPE Report version 1** — comments on findings and tentative conclusions (lesson 4.4.2.1).</Item>
  <Item>**Recommendations workshop** — co-creation of the final recommendations (lesson 4.3).</Item>
</Checklist>

<h3>How members are invited</h3>

Once identified, the UNFPA government partner — or the CO Representative on the government partner's behalf — sends a formal invitation to the proposed ERG members. The invitation includes:

1. The CPE draft ToR.
2. The **ToR for the ERG**, prepared by the CPE Manager.

<InPractice country="From the handbook">
The ERG is not a rubber-stamp body. The handbook expects substantive technical review — particularly on the theory of change critical analysis and the evaluation matrix at design-report stage. CPE Managers who pack the ERG with sympathetic insiders are reducing the credibility of their own evaluation.
</InPractice>

<Callout variant="warn" title="The ERG advises — it does not approve">
The ERG provides comments and technical advice. **Approval** of CPE deliverables sits with the CPE Manager (with the RO M&E Adviser) and ultimately the CO Representative — not with the ERG. This keeps the evaluation team's independent judgement intact.
</Callout>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="ERG">Evaluation Reference Group — multi-stakeholder body that reviews every major CPE deliverable, advises on methodology, and co-creates the recommendations.</Term>
  <Term name="ERG ToR">A separate Terms of Reference for the ERG itself, prepared by the CPE Manager and sent with the formal invitation to members.</Term>
  <Term name="Co-creation">The handbook's term for the multi-party process by which recommendations are drafted by the team, reviewed and reshaped by the ERG, then consolidated — not handed down by the team alone (lesson 4.3).</Term>
</KeyTerms>
