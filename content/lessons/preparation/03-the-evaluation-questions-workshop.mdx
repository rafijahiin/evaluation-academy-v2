<Callout variant="key" title="What the workshop is for">
Undertaking a CPE is about **asking questions that matter**. The evaluation questions workshop is where the CO opens up the country programme's theory of change for collective reflection — and reduces it to a focused set of 6–8 questions worth investigating.
</Callout>

To be relevant and useful, the CPE must focus on issues reflecting the perspective, insight and information needs of people with diverse responsibilities in the design and implementation of UNFPA interventions, as well as their intended beneficiaries. The CO Representative calls the workshop; the CPE Manager organises and leads it.

<h3>Who participates</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="CO staff">Relevant programme, operations, and communication staff.</KV>

  <KV title="Select stakeholders">Government partners; implementing partners; development partners; representatives of rights-holders' organisations (with attention to marginalised groups and youth).</KV>

  <KV title="Regional office">RO M&E Adviser and select RO technical advisers.</KV>

  <KV title="Convener vs facilitator">The CO Representative calls the workshop. The CPE Manager organises and leads.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>Reality-checking the theory of change</h3>

The workshop opens a discussion on the country programme's **theory of change**. Is there common agreement on whether it is well articulated? Does it lead to well-defined outcomes? Are the assumptions and external factors realistic? Subjecting the theory of change to this reality check — four or five years after it was designed — identifies possible gaps, weaknesses, blind spots and exaggerated expectations.

Participants should reflect on issues such as:

- **Is the theory of change known by all, understood, and currently used?** If not used, why? If used, what has been learned?
- **Is there agreement about intended results**, or do different stakeholders have different views?
- **How well does the theory of change make explicit the change theories underpinning it?** For example, increased uptake of family planning services can come from one or more theories: changing social norms; capacity development for health providers; removal of barriers, etc.
- **Where does the causal chain seem to break?** Where does achieving a particular intermediate outcome not seem to lead to the next?
- **Has context changed significantly** (e.g. humanitarian crisis, policy reform), and how does it affect the theory of change?
- **Have reviews or monitoring missions** highlighted issues the CPE needs to probe further?

<h3>Game changers and accelerators</h3>

Participants should also consider the **drivers of change** to realise the UNFPA transformative results in the country: human-rights based and gender transformative approaches; innovation and digitalisation; partnership and South-to-South cooperation; financing; data and evidence; **leave no one behind** (LNOB); resilience and adaptation; complementarity across development, humanitarian, and peace-responsive efforts.

<InPractice country="From Box 4 of the handbook">
The three transformative results that UNFPA committed to achieving by 2030 anchor every CPE: **(1) End the unmet need for family planning, including modern methods of contraception. (2) End preventable maternal deaths. (3) End gender-based violence and harmful practices, including female genital mutilation and child marriage.**
</InPractice>

<h3>What makes a good evaluation question</h3>

Once consensus emerges on key topics, participants must collectively prioritise. Evaluation questions are organised around the OECD-DAC criteria (relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability — and for humanitarian programmes, coverage and connectedness; see lesson 2.2.2).

The handbook states that questions must be **relevant, useful, feasible, and inclusive**:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Relevant">Each question grounded in the country programme — not a copy from other CPEs. Of direct interest to UNFPA, its partners and the country programme stakeholders.</KV>

  <KV title="Useful">Questions address high-level or strategic themes, and help resolve decisions in the next country programme cycle. Responses provide information for evidence-based decision-making.</KV>

  <KV title="Feasible">Within the evaluators' ability given the CPE methodology, time, and funds. Realistic about scope so data collection and analysis can be completed.</KV>

  <KV title="Inclusive">Reflecting LNOB and rights-based approaches. Questions address marginalised and vulnerable groups; responses generate learning on how to meet their needs in the next cycle.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>Narrowing down to 6–8 questions</h3>

To finalise the list, workshop participants ask:

<Checklist storageKey="ch1-l3-eq-prioritisation" title="Prioritising the evaluation questions">
  <Item>Does the question focus on a critical or major issue — or is it merely of interest at this specific point in time?</Item>
  <Item>Does it focus on higher-level transformative results rather than activities implementation and output targets?</Item>
  <Item>Will the CPE purpose and objectives be compromised if this question is dropped?</Item>
  <Item>Is it possible to adequately answer the question within the timeframe and budget limits?</Item>
  <Item>Who will use the response? Not all stakeholders are in a position to use the information from every question.</Item>
  <Item>Will the question provide information that can be acted upon to improve the next programming cycle?</Item>
  <Item>Is the response not already known? Is another evaluation likely to provide the answer?</Item>
</Checklist>

<Callout variant="tip" title="Workshop participation creates downstream cooperation">
Having stakeholders participate in formulating questions also fosters their cooperation later — during data collection, in providing feedback on the design report and CPE report, and in co-creating the recommendations (lesson 4.3).
</Callout>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Theory of change">The articulated causal chain — from UNFPA inputs to activities, outputs, outcomes, and the three transformative results — including the assumptions and external factors at each link.</Term>
  <Term name="OECD-DAC criteria">The five evaluation criteria adopted by the OECD Development Assistance Committee: relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability.</Term>
  <Term name="Transformative results">UNFPA's three world-changing commitments by 2030: zero unmet need for family planning; zero preventable maternal death; zero gender-based violence.</Term>
  <Term name="LNOB">"Leave No One Behind" — the 2030 Agenda principle of reaching the furthest left behind first.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
