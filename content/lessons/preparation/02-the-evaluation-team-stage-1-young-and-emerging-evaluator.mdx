<Callout variant="key" title="Stage 1 first, before the main team">
Assembling the CPE evaluation team follows a **two-stage process**. The first stage is the recruitment of a **Young and Emerging Evaluator (YEE)** — initiated at the beginning of the preparation phase, immediately after the CPE launch meeting.
</Callout>

The YEE is brought on board well before the main team. Their early engagement has three significant advantages:

1. They support the CPE Manager in the **numerous and time-consuming activities of the preparation phase**.
2. Joining the CO during preparation gives the YEE time to get a thorough orientation to the UNFPA mandate, the country programme, the UN system, and the CPE methodology.
3. It signals UNFPA's commitment to **youth participation in evaluation** — central to the *Leveraging the Power of Youth in Evaluation* agenda.

<h3>Who is a YEE?</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Profile">A junior professional below 35 years of age with less than 5 years of professional experience.</KV>

  <KV title="Skills">Well-versed in evaluation methods and new technologies — including the use of AI in data management and analysis.</KV>

  <KV title="Value to the CPE">External perspective; innovative ideas; lower fee expectations than senior consultants; direct support to the CPE Manager during preparation.</KV>

  <KV title="Value to the YEE">A unique opportunity to put knowledge into practice, consolidate evaluation skills, contribute to national evaluation capacities.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>How the YEE is engaged across all phases</h3>

Once the main team is recruited at stage 2 (lesson 1.6), the CPE Manager and the team leader reflect on how to meaningfully engage the YEE across the evaluation — design, fieldwork, reporting, dissemination — as well as how they will ensure **dual supervision** and undertake a mentoring role throughout the process. In consultation with the YEE, and based on their skills, experience and qualifications, the CPE Manager and team leader define the YEE's role and responsibilities to provide opportunities for learning and growth.

<Callout variant="warn" title="The YEE is not a thematic expert">
Under no circumstances shall the YEE be hired as a thematic expert covering a programmatic area of the CPE. The full requirements of the team leader and each team member are spelled out in R2U ToR sections 12.1 and 12.2.
</Callout>

<h3>The recruitment process</h3>

The CPE Manager ensures the YEE selection and recruitment follows a **transparent and competitive process** that is inclusive of youth from groups that are least represented or left behind.

<Checklist storageKey="ch1-l2-yee-recruitment" title="YEE recruitment steps">
  <Item>CO Representative approves the YEE Terms of Reference (template in the CPE Management Kit).</Item>
  <Item>CPE Manager, in consultation with CO operations staff, publishes a call for consultancy for young and emerging evaluators.</Item>
  <Item>Selection panel reviews candidates after closing of the call.</Item>
  <Item>CPE Manager organises interviews with shortlisted candidates.</Item>
  <Item>Recruitment of the selected YEE.</Item>
</Checklist>

<InPractice country="Field perspective">
Treat the YEE as a colleague from day one. If they spend the preparation phase only filing documents, they will not be ready to contribute to data collection or analysis when the field phase begins. Build their orientation into the time schedule — and give them visible, substantive work.
</InPractice>
