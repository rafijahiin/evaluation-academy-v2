<p>The CO Representative nominates the <strong>CPE Manager</strong> — usually the country office M&E Officer, or a CO staff member designated in consultation with the Regional Office M&E Adviser. The CPE Manager then leads the rest of preparation.</p>

<p>The launch meeting brings together all CO staff and the RO M&E Adviser. The CPE Manager presents the <strong>CPE Roadmap</strong> — the milestones diagram that takes everyone through the five phases: Preparation, Design, Fieldwork, Reporting, Dissemination & Facilitation of Use.</p>

<h3>What the launch meeting must accomplish</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Shared understanding">The Ready-to-Use (R2U) ToR template is shared and presented to all CO staff.</KV>

  <KV title="Roles and contributions">Clarity on who contributes what to the ToR, annexes, document repository — and by when.</KV>

  <KV title="ERG membership">The role of the Evaluation Reference Group is explained; its membership is discussed and agreed.</KV>

  <KV title="Team profile">The composition of the external evaluation team is agreed (and the option of contracting a firm vs individual consultants is examined).</KV>

  <KV title="Selection panel">The composition of the panel to select the consultants is set out.</KV>

  <KV title="Timeline">The CPE timeline is fixed — minimum 11 months, so the report is ready in time to inform the next country programme document.</KV>

  <KV title="EQ workshop date">Participants and date for the upcoming evaluation questions workshop are announced.</KV>

</KVGrid>

<Callout variant="key" title="11-month minimum">
The handbook says explicitly: allow at least 11 months end-to-end, so that if unforeseen delays occur, you can still meet the final deadline and inform the next programming cycle.
</Callout>

<p>After the launch, the government partner (or the CO Representative on the government's behalf) circulates an official announcement letter to inform all key stakeholders that the CPE has begun.</p>
