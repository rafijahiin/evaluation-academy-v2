<Callout variant="key" title="Two distinct meetings, two distinct purposes">
**Induction meeting:** the CPE Manager + the evaluation team — internal team alignment on the ToR, methodology, governance, timeline, ethics.

**Orientation meeting:** the evaluation team + the entire CO — the country office presents itself, the country programme, and the context to the team.
</Callout>

<h3>Remote, in-person, or hybrid?</h3>

Depending on the country's context and circumstances — cost implications, environmental footprint, availability of the international consultant to travel — the CPE Manager, after discussion with the CO Representative and in consultation with the team leader, may elect to organise part or all of the design phase **remotely**.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="If remote">The CPE Manager (with the YEE and relevant CO staff) ensures all activities and actions can take place using virtual team communication tools — video conferencing, file storage and collaboration tools.</KV>

  <KV title="If in-country">The CPE Manager must ensure the evaluators have adequate working space and basic facilities (internet, printer access) in the CO.</KV>

  <KV title="Hybrid pattern">Locally recruited consultants (team members and YEE) are in-country; the team leader (often recruited internationally) could lead the design phase remotely and join the team in person for the field phase.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>The induction meeting</h3>

The CPE Manager welcomes the evaluation team and ensures they have a clear understanding of their assignment as well as easy access to all the documentation necessary for the design phase.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Main focus">Open discussion on the CPE ToR (including annexes) and the UNFPA CPE methodological guidance — ensuring a common understanding of objectives, process, and approach.</KV>

  <KV title="Methodology check">The CPE Manager and team leader verify all team members are well versed in the UNFPA methodology, providing additional explanation and support where needed.</KV>

  <KV title="Governance">The CPE Manager explains the CPE governance structure and quality assurance process, including the role, responsibilities, and membership of the ERG.</KV>

  <KV title="CO structure briefing">The CPE Manager presents the CO organogram with a detailed list of CO staff (including sub-offices), with contact information and position.</KV>

  <KV title="Schedule agreement">The evaluators and the CPE Manager agree on a tentative timeline for the design phase, the submission of the design report (version 1), and the first ERG meeting.</KV>

  <KV title="Ethics">The CPE Manager shares the UNEG Code of conduct for the evaluators' review and signature.</KV>

  <KV title="Internal team setup">The team leader (in concertation with the CPE Manager) assigns roles and responsibilities — who will draft which sections of the design report, bearing in mind days allocated. Rules of interaction (communication within team, with CPE Manager, with other CO staff) are set.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<h3>The orientation meeting</h3>

The orientation meeting brings together (virtually and / or in the CO premises) the CO staff and the evaluation team. The CPE Manager (in collaboration with relevant CO staff) prepares a presentation and invites the respective CO programme leads and operations staff to present the slides relevant to their areas of responsibility.

<Checklist storageKey="ch2-l1-orientation-presentation" title="Box 3 — Presentation made by CO staff to the evaluation team">
  <Item>Objectives of the CPE.</Item>
  <Item>Geographical map of UNFPA programme areas.</Item>
  <Item>Roadmap of the CPE (with indicative dates for each deliverable and meeting).</Item>
  <Item>Main roles and responsibilities of CPE Manager, evaluation team, CO staff, RO M&E Adviser, IEO.</Item>
  <Item>The ERG: composition and responsibilities.</Item>
  <Item>Theory of change of the country programme.</Item>
  <Item>The CPE approach: a contribution analysis based on the country programme theory of change.</Item>
  <Item>Overview of each country programme thematic area (outputs, contribution to outcomes, progress, challenges).</Item>
  <Item>Cross-cutting issues: human rights-based approach, gender equality, LNOB and reaching the furthest behind.</Item>
  <Item>Overview of CO management structure (including sub-offices).</Item>
  <Item>Overview of the country programme financial structure (budget/expenditure by year, by thematic area, by core/non-core, by partner, by gender contribution).</Item>
  <Item>Preliminary evaluation questions.</Item>
  <Item>The evaluation matrix.</Item>
  <Item>What the CPE is NOT — stressing that the CPE is not an assessment of activities implementation or outputs achievements.</Item>
  <Item>The CPE stakeholder map.</Item>
  <Item>Structure of the design report.</Item>
  <Item>Next steps in the design phase.</Item>
</Checklist>

<InPractice country="From the handbook">
Box 3 ends with "What the CPE is **not**" — a deliberately framed slide. The handbook is explicit that the CPE is not an audit, not an activity-implementation review, and not an output-counting exercise. Without this framing up front, evaluators sometimes drift toward describing what was delivered instead of analysing what changed.
</InPractice>

<Callout variant="tip" title="Preparing for the orientation">
The evaluators should identify the key issues and questions to raise during the orientation meeting — it marks their first interaction with the CO senior management and personnel. Coming prepared signals competence and shapes how the CO will engage with the team for the rest of the CPE.
</Callout>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Induction">Team-internal alignment meeting between the CPE Manager and the evaluation team — ToR, methodology, governance, ethics, schedule.</Term>
  <Term name="Orientation">Wider meeting where the country office presents itself and the country programme to the evaluation team (Box 3 outline).</Term>
  <Term name="UNEG Code of conduct">The UN Evaluation Group's Code of Conduct for Evaluation in the UN System — signed by every evaluator on the team.</Term>
  <Term name="Hybrid design phase">The team leader leads parts of design remotely while the national team and YEE work in-country, with the leader joining in person for the field phase.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
