<Callout variant="key" title="The central tenet of the CPE">
The central tenet of the CPE is the country programme **theory of change** and the analysis of its **logic and internal coherence**. The evaluation team reviews the logical framework as set up at the beginning of the programme cycle and analyses the intervention logic — how the programme is expected to bring about the intended results.
</Callout>

<h3>What evaluators look at as they analyse the theory of change</h3>

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Context">The context in which the interventions take place.</KV>

  <KV title="Needs and problems">The needs to be met and the problems to be addressed by the programme.</KV>

  <KV title="Engagement and activities">The modes of engagement and the nature of implemented activities.</KV>

  <KV title="Results chain">The steps in the results chain — outputs and subsequent outcomes.</KV>

  <KV title="Assumptions and risks">The cause-and-effect assumptions behind the various links, and the risks to those assumptions.</KV>

  <KV title="External factors">Other external factors that may affect the results — political, economic, demographic, climate, etc.</KV>
</KVGrid>

Their comprehension of the theory of change is **instrumental for guiding data collection tools and analysis methods**, and assembling evidence about UNFPA's contribution to the various levels of intended changes.

<h3>Why contribution, not attribution</h3>

Contribution analysis is based on the recognition that **proving attribution is often not feasible or practical**. The CPE works in a complex, dynamic country context. Multiple actors are at work; UNFPA is one of them. The evaluators cannot run a controlled experiment that isolates UNFPA's effect from everything else.

What evaluators **can** do is assess causal connections — consider progress towards outcomes and come to **credible conclusions about the contribution made by UNFPA** to the observed results. The contribution analysis also guides their analysis of **how and why** the UNFPA interventions have made a difference — or not — and **for whom**.

<InPractice country="Box 4 — UNFPA theory of change and the three transformative results">
The theory of change addresses why and how change happens in the context of the UNFPA strategic plan 2022-2025. It presents a holistic view of the causal and enabling conditions that must be in place to achieve the three transformative results that UNFPA committed to by 2030:
**(1) End the unmet need for family planning, including modern methods of contraception. (2) End preventable maternal deaths. (3) End gender-based violence and harmful practices, including female genital mutilation and child marriage.**

The theory of change also cites the research and evidence underpinning the relationships between these conditions and the three transformative results, and spells out the risks and assumptions that may impede the results chain from occurring.
</InPractice>

<h3>The five conditions for inferring causality (Mayne)</h3>

Adopting Mayne's contribution analysis framework allows the team to explore attribution **through assessing contribution**. Causality is inferred when:

<Checklist storageKey="ch2-l3-causality-conditions" title="Mayne's five conditions for credible contribution">
  <Item>The country programme is based on a **reasoned theory of change** — the assumptions behind why the programme is expected to work are sound, plausible, and agreed upon by at least some key players.</Item>
  <Item>The **modes of engagement are appropriate** — the chosen instruments (advocacy, capacity building, service delivery, etc.) fit the change the programme is trying to produce.</Item>
  <Item>The **activities of the programme were implemented** — what was planned was actually delivered.</Item>
  <Item>The country programme theory of change is **verified by evidence** — the chain of expected results actually occurred.</Item>
  <Item>**Other factors influencing the programme were assessed** — and were either shown not to have made a significant contribution, or, if they did, their relative contribution was explicitly recognised.</Item>
</Checklist>

<h3>What evaluators are looking for</h3>

With a contribution analysis, evaluators assess the extent to which the UNFPA country programme (or components of it) **made a difference** — being a necessary part of a broader package of factors that together brought about or contributed to important changes.

<Callout variant="key" title="Box 5 — Key features of the change story (Strategic plan, 2022–2025)">
Ten features of the UNFPA strategic plan's "change story" should bear on every CPE:

1. Anchoring the theory of change on the three transformative results.
2. Factoring a human-rights based approach (non-discrimination, accountability, quality of services).
3. Including structural barriers / root causes that can impede results.
4. "Many-to-many" relationships between outputs and outcomes for integrated programming.
5. Fewer determinants more directly influencing the transformative results.
6. Intermediate results between outcomes and outputs to complete the results pathway.
7. Integrating lessons from COVID-19.
8. Using a storytelling approach to communicate the change story.
9. Introducing a set of **accelerators** that enhance performance.
10. Aligning with the 2020 UN Quadrennial Comprehensive Policy Review (QCPR).
</Callout>

<h3>The theory of change as continuous thread</h3>

During the CPE, the evaluators **return to the country programme theory of change** to verify how it actually works in practice, identify limitations, and reflect on how it might be strengthened. They may also identify **unintended consequences** — positive or negative — arising from the achievement of outputs and contribution to outcomes.

The theory of change plays a central role **throughout the evaluation process**: from design, through data collection, into reporting (findings, conclusions, recommendations). The design report dedicates a specific section to the evaluators' detailed analysis of the country programme theory of change (lesson 2.4).

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Contribution analysis">An evaluation approach (Mayne 2008) for assessing causal connections when controlled experiments aren't feasible. Builds a credible contribution argument rather than claiming sole attribution.</Term>
  <Term name="Theory of change">The articulated causal chain — inputs through to transformative results — with explicit assumptions and external factors at each link.</Term>
  <Term name="Intervention logic">The pathway of how activities are expected to produce outputs, contribute to outcomes, and eventually transformative results.</Term>
  <Term name="Causal assumption">An "if X, then Y" hypothesis embedded in the theory of change — the evaluation matrix tests these.</Term>
  <Term name="Accelerators">Cross-cutting drivers in the UNFPA strategic plan: human-rights and gender transformative approaches; innovation and digitalisation; partnerships and South-South cooperation; financing; data and evidence; LNOB; resilience and adaptation; humanitarian-development-peace nexus.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
