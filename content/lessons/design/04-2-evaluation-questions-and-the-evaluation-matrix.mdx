<Callout variant="key" title="From preliminary questions to a fixed matrix">
The **preliminary** evaluation questions developed by the CO (in the ToR section 5) are **further specified** by the evaluation team during the design phase. Each question gets its assumptions, indicators, methods — assembled into the evaluation matrix. **At design report v2 approval, the matrix is fixed** and cannot be amended during fieldwork.
</Callout>

<h3>The OECD-DAC evaluation criteria</h3>

The evaluation questions address **five OECD-DAC criteria** — the foundation of every UNFPA CPE:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Relevance">The extent to which the intervention objectives and design respond to rights-holders, country, and partner/institution needs, policies, and priorities — and continue to do so if circumstances change.</KV>

  <KV title="Coherence">The compatibility of the intervention with other interventions in the country, sector, or institution. Applies both within UNFPA (linkages between SRHR and GBV programming, etc.) and with projects of other UN agencies, INGOs, and development partners.</KV>

  <KV title="Effectiveness">The extent to which the intervention achieved, or is expected to achieve, its objectives and results — including any differential results across groups.</KV>

  <KV title="Efficiency">The extent to which the intervention delivers, or is likely to deliver, results in an economic and timely way. Could the same results have been achieved with fewer financial or technical resources?</KV>

  <KV title="Sustainability">The extent to which the net benefits of the intervention continue, or are likely to continue, even if or when the intervention ends.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Why impact is NOT a CPE criterion">
**Impact (the sixth DAC criterion) is beyond the scope of the CPE.** Assessing impact requires a different time frame and a different methodology. However, by assessing the contribution of programme outputs to outcomes, the CPE will highlight the extent to which the programme contributes to UNFPA transformative results.
</Callout>

<h3>Humanitarian +2: coverage and connectedness</h3>

For country programmes that include humanitarian and/or emergency interventions, **two additional criteria** apply:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Coverage">The extent to which major population groups facing life-threatening conditions were reached by humanitarian action. Evaluators assess inclusion bias (people receiving support who shouldn't have) and exclusion bias (groups who should have been covered but weren't) — disaggregated by sex, socio-economic grouping, and ethnicity.</KV>

  <KV title="Connectedness">The extent to which activities of a short-term emergency nature are carried out in a context that takes longer-term and interconnected problems into account — a nexus approach — and that also indicates the complementarity of UNFPA with other partner interventions.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Scope limits in humanitarian assessment">
The assessment of humanitarian interventions within a CPE is possible **only for crises that are geographically localised and/or limited in time and intensity**. Large-scale and/or protracted humanitarian crises require a specific methodological approach (ALNAP Evaluation of Humanitarian Action Guide, 2016; UNFPA guidance on humanitarian evaluations).
</Callout>

<h3>Assumptions for verification</h3>

At the design phase, each evaluation question must be accompanied by **assumptions for verification**. These encapsulate how the country programme is expected to influence or contribute to the intended results. For each question — based on the team's understanding of the theory of change — evaluators formulate assumptions that are, in effect, **hypotheses to be tested through data collection**.

As they document the assumptions, the evaluators will be able to:
- Explain **why and the extent** to which the interventions did (or did not) lead toward the expected outcomes.
- Identify the **critical elements** to success.
- Pinpoint **other external factors** that have influenced the programme and contributed to change.

> **Note from the handbook:** Each evaluation question must include a minimum of **two assumptions** to be tested for their validity in the implementation of the country programme.

<h3>The evaluation matrix template (Table 5)</h3>

The evaluation matrix unpacks each evaluation question and specifies four things:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Evaluation criteria">Which of the five DAC criteria (and humanitarian +2 where applicable) the question addresses.</KV>

  <KV title="Assumptions for verification">The hypotheses to be tested — at least two per question.</KV>

  <KV title="Indicators">Quantitative and qualitative indicators that will verify or refute each assumption.</KV>

  <KV title="Methods and tools for data collection">How the data will be gathered — KIIs, FGDs, surveys, document review, observation.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<InPractice country="Example from Table 5 — maternal health">
**EQ1:** "To what extent has the UNFPA maternal health programme contributed to ensuring the education, training, and deployment of an adequately skilled, motivated and sustainable midwifery workforce?"

**Related criteria:** Relevance, effectiveness, efficiency.

**Assumption 1.1:** UNFPA maternal health programme inputs regarding norms, standards, policies and guidelines relating to midwifery contribute to a more enabled policy and regulatory environment for midwives.

**Indicators (1.1):** National policies, strategies and plans to govern midwifery practice and workforce capacity development; strengthened regulatory bodies; policies and standards on supportive supervision, mentorship, and a safe work environment.

**Methods:** Interviews with UNFPA MH/SRHR staff; interviews with Ministry of Health and Midwives' Association; documents review (national policy, strategy, guidelines).
</InPractice>

<h3>Realistic feasibility check</h3>

The evaluation team — together with the CPE Manager and with support from RO M&E Adviser as needed — must reflect on whether the questions (as proposed in the ToR) can realistically be addressed within the time and resources allocated:

<Checklist storageKey="ch2-l4-feasibility" title="Feasibility check for each evaluation question">
  <Item>What data is available in secondary sources — documentation, CO internal monitoring and information systems?</Item>
  <Item>How extensive is the data needed to address the question?</Item>
  <Item>Has sufficient time elapsed for certain interventions to demonstrate tangible effects?</Item>
  <Item>How feasible is it to access and exploit primary sources within the schedule?</Item>
  <Item>Are key informants accessible? Will logistics support agile data collection?</Item>
</Checklist>

<h3>What happens to the matrix during fieldwork</h3>

The evaluation questions are agreed in the design report (as formally approved by the CPE Manager). Fine-tuning of assumptions and/or indicators can still happen at the start of the field phase based on initial data. **But during the field phase, the evaluation matrix is fixed and cannot be amended.**

As they move forward in data collection, the evaluators populate the matrix with qualitative and quantitative data obtained through interviews, on-site observations, surveys, and continuing document review. The completed evaluation matrix is **annexed to the final evaluation report (annex 1)**.

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Evaluation matrix">The CPE's analytical framework: a structured table where each question is unpacked into criteria, assumptions, indicators, and methods.</Term>
  <Term name="Assumption for verification">An "if X, then Y" hypothesis derived from the theory of change — the building block that gets tested through data collection.</Term>
  <Term name="DAC criteria">The five OECD Development Assistance Committee criteria: relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability. Impact is excluded from CPE scope.</Term>
  <Term name="Coverage / Connectedness">The two additional criteria applied to humanitarian components within a CPE (assuming the humanitarian situation is geographically localised and time-limited).</Term>
</KeyTerms>
