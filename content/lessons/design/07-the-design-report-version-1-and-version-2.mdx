<Callout variant="key" title="Purpose of the design report">
The design report ensures there is a **common understanding** — among the evaluation team, CPE Manager, CO senior management and staff, and the ERG — of the purpose and scope of the CPE (and its possible limitations). With its approval, all parties endorse: the evaluation questions and assumptions; the matrix; the methodology and main research tools; the stakeholder sample and selected sites; the work plan and timeline.

The design report also gives the CPE Manager and ERG a chance to check the evaluators' **level of understanding** of the country context as well as the UN and UNFPA response.
</Callout>

The drafting follows a process **punctuated by two in-depth quality-assurance stages** performed on two versions.

<h3>Version 1 — drafting and team-internal QA</h3>

Under the guidance of the team leader, evaluation team members write their respective contributions to the first version of the design report, following the outline and recommended page length (section 2.4.4 of the handbook; 55 pages maximum excluding opening pages and annexes).

The **team leader** assembles and finalises the report, ensuring it meets the quality criteria spelt out in:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Guidance on evaluation quality assurance">The criteria relevant for the design report — specifically design and methodology criteria.</KV>

  <KV title="Quality-assurance checklist (Box 8)">The 9-item handbook checklist that the team leader works through before submitting to the CPE Manager.</KV>
</KVGrid>

After a thorough internal quality check, the team leader shares the report with the CPE Manager.

<h3>CPE Manager + RO M&E Adviser QA</h3>

The CPE Manager conducts an in-depth quality control of the design report and shares it with the **regional M&E Adviser** for review and guidance.

- **Where improvements are needed**, the CPE Manager requests a revision of the design report, stating those specific changes that need to be made by the team — and why.
- **When version 1 passes the quality control**, the CPE Manager shares it with the ERG members.

<h3>Box 8 — Design report quality-assurance checklist</h3>

<Checklist storageKey="ch2-l7-design-qa" title="Box 8 — Quality-assurance checklist (CPE Manager → ERG)">
  <Item>Have the evaluators correctly understood why UNFPA is doing this evaluation? Have they correctly interpreted the purpose and objectives?</Item>
  <Item>Have the evaluators correctly understood what is being evaluated?</Item>
  <Item>Does the report show the evaluators have reviewed the entire country programme and its components?</Item>
  <Item>Have they analysed the country programme theory of change, its logic and internal coherence?</Item>
  <Item>Have they reviewed what elements of the country context are important to consider in the evaluation?</Item>
  <Item>Do the evaluation questions, the assumptions for verification, and related indicators adequately take into account the relevant aspects of the programme?</Item>
  <Item>Have the evaluators convincingly explained how they intend to carry out the evaluation?</Item>
  <Item>Does the design report present a convincing evaluation methodology and approach? Are the tools for data collection adequate?</Item>
  <Item>Have the evaluators identified adequate informants (annex 3) and sites to collect required information with a view to answering the evaluation questions? Does the report identify expected risks and constraints, with viable options to minimise their effects?</Item>
</Checklist>

The CPE Manager **shares this checklist with the ERG members** when circulating the draft design report — making the QA standard transparent and shared.

<h3>The ERG meeting</h3>

In anticipation of the ERG meeting, the CPE Manager circulates the design report. When tabling it, the CPE Manager calls ERG members' attention to specific sections and seeks their input on:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Theory of change analysis">The evaluators' review and critical analysis of the country programme theory of change.</KV>

  <KV title="Evaluation matrix">ERG input is particularly important here — the matrix will shape every field interaction.</KV>

  <KV title="Country context and UN/UNFPA response">Chapters for fact-checking by ERG members close to programme history.</KV>

  <KV title="Methodology (chapters 4 and 5)">ERG members with evaluation methodology expertise are directed to dedicate attention to these chapters specifically.</KV>
</KVGrid>

The team leader (with contributions from all team members and the CPE Manager) prepares a presentation for the ERG meeting.

<h3>Box 9 — Suggested presentation outline for the ERG</h3>

<Checklist storageKey="ch2-l7-erg-presentation" title="Box 9 — Suggested presentation outline to the ERG">
  <Item>Introduction.</Item>
  <Item>Purpose and objectives of the evaluation.</Item>
  <Item>Evaluation roadmap (phases of the evaluation with timeline).</Item>
  <Item>The evaluation approach: in-depth analysis of the country programme theory of change.</Item>
  <Item>Main evaluation criteria and refined evaluation questions with assumptions for verification.</Item>
  <Item>Evaluation matrix — structure and purpose.</Item>
  <Item>Data collection tools.</Item>
  <Item>Stakeholder sampling and sites selection.</Item>
  <Item>Evaluation report structure.</Item>
  <Item>Provisional timelines for key deliverables.</Item>
  <Item>Roles and responsibilities.</Item>
  <Item>Immediate next steps.</Item>
</Checklist>

The team leader must ensure all team members are ready to respond to questions and comments from the ERG relating to their respective areas of responsibility and expertise. The **CO Representative chairs** the ERG meeting and moderates the discussion. The **CPE Manager** keeps track of all comments — expressed during the meeting or sent in writing — and compiles them for sharing with the team.

<h3>Version 2 — revision and final approval</h3>

The evaluation team takes stock of the comments received and revises the design report to produce **version 2**, submitted to the CPE Manager for final review.

- The CPE Manager shares v2 with the RO M&E Adviser.
- In consultation with the RO M&E Adviser, the CPE Manager conducts an in-depth quality control.
- **Where improvements are needed**, the CPE Manager requests a revision, stating specific changes.
- **When v2 passes the quality control**, the CPE Manager — in consultation with the RO M&E Adviser and the CO Representative — **formally approves the design report**, whose content is now considered final.

<InPractice country="From the handbook">
After v2 approval, the **evaluation matrix is fixed**. The questions, the assumptions, the indicators, the methods — all locked. From this moment, the team enters fieldwork with a defined analytical framework. Discovering, mid-fieldwork, that a key assumption needs reframing requires a deliberate decision (and CPE Manager + ERG sign-off) — not a quiet rewrite of the matrix.
</InPractice>

<h3>Structure of the design report (section 2.4.4)</h3>

The maximum length is **55 pages** (excluding opening pages and annexes). Indicative chapter lengths:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Opening pages (6–8 pp, Roman numerals)">Cover; country map of UNFPA programme areas; CPE team table; ERG table; table of contents; tables and figures list; acronyms; key facts table.</KV>

  <KV title="Chapter 1 — Introduction (2–4 pp)">Purpose and objectives of the evaluation; thematic, geographic and temporal scope; purpose of the design report.</KV>

  <KV title="Chapter 2 — Country context (4–6 pp)">Development challenges and national strategies; the role of external assistance.</KV>

  <KV title="Chapter 3 — UN and UNFPA response">UN and UNFPA strategic response; the country programme; theory of change.</KV>

  <KV title="Chapter 4 — Evaluation approach and methods">DAC criteria; evaluation questions and assumptions; evaluation matrix; mixed methods; data collection tools.</KV>

  <KV title="Chapter 5 — Sampling, sites, and agenda">Stakeholder sampling rationale; site selection rationale; field-phase agenda (annex 6).</KV>

  <KV title="Annexes">Evaluation matrix (annex 1); data collection tools (annex 1); stakeholder sample map (annex 3); field-phase agenda (annex 6); etc.</KV>
</KVGrid>
