<Callout variant="key" title="Desk review is the bulk of the design phase">
The handbook is explicit: at the design phase, **extensive document review forms the bulk of the evaluators' work**. This is not a quick scan — it is a structured deep read of the country programme as it was planned, monitored, and reported.
</Callout>

<h3>What the evaluators read</h3>

The desk review begins with the **Country Programme Document (CPD)** and an in-depth analysis of the country programme **theory of change** and integrated results and resources framework. From there:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Planning documents">CO annual work plans (AWPs) — both original and revised versions. Implementing partner AWPs.</KV>

  <KV title="Self-reporting">Country Office Annual Reports (COARs) for the entire period under evaluation.</KV>

  <KV title="Previous evaluations">Earlier CPE reports, project evaluations, mid-term reviews, research studies — all reviewed for what they already established.</KV>

  <KV title="Project documentation">Project documents, progress and monitoring reports, field mission reports (often contain a wealth of action points).</KV>

  <KV title="Donor and joint reports">Donor reports, joint programme reports, implementing partner work plan progress reports.</KV>

  <KV title="Technical and audit">Technical studies and reports, statistical reports, audit reports.</KV>

  <KV title="National household surveys">Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS), Multiple Indicator Cluster Surveys (MICS), and other major national surveys.</KV>

  <KV title="UNFPA centralized evaluations">UNFPA Independent Evaluation Office centralized evaluations germane to the country programme thematic scope.</KV>
</KVGrid>

The key documents for review are provided in the **online document repository** set up by the CPE Manager (lesson 1.7). As they move forward in the design and field phases, the evaluators complement this initial batch with additional documents they identify or that the CO and partners bring to their attention — relevant evaluations by other UN agencies, INGOs, or partner organisations.

<h3>First round of interviews</h3>

The evaluators must complement their document review with **interviews (or group discussions)** with:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="CO senior management">CO Representative; Deputy Representative / Assistant Representative; programme officers across thematic areas.</KV>

  <KV title="Members of the ERG">Key stakeholders in the country programme — drawn from the ERG composition.</KV>

  <KV title="Key stakeholders as appropriate">Government partners coordinating specific outputs; selected implementing partners with deep programme history.</KV>
</KVGrid>

The evaluators must talk with stakeholders about **how they understand UNFPA interventions work** — or are intended to work — what are the intended outcomes and how stakeholders think they may be achieved.

<h3>Why this combination matters</h3>

An in-depth document review **further informed by interviews with people involved in the design, management and supervision of the country programme interventions** constitutes the requisite steps for the evaluation team to:

1. Understand the **logic of the UNFPA interventions** — how each output was supposed to connect to each outcome.
2. Undertake a **critical review of the country programme theory of change** as set up at the beginning of the programme cycle — identifying assumptions, gaps, weaknesses.

<Callout variant="warn" title="A common drift: outputs measured but not analysed">
Evaluators must be well aware that UNFPA COARs and other monitoring documents mostly inform **activities** — numbers of midwives trained, upgraded health centres, equipment supplied — and largely do not adequately capture the extent to which **outputs are achieved**, and most importantly the **extent of their contribution to outcomes**. The CPE is interested in results, not delivery. Interviews and (later) field data must close that gap.
</Callout>

<InPractice country="From the handbook">
A four-year country programme typically generates hundreds of documents in the repository — strategic, operational, financial, partner-submitted. The evaluators don't read every page of every document. They prioritise by relevance to the preliminary evaluation questions, leaving the granular tracing of activities for triangulation later.
</InPractice>

<Checklist storageKey="ch2-l2-desk-review" title="Desk review readiness checklist">
  <Item>CPD and theory of change read in full and annotated for assumptions to test.</Item>
  <Item>All AWPs (original and revised) reviewed for the evaluation period.</Item>
  <Item>All COARs reviewed for the evaluation period.</Item>
  <Item>Previous CPEs and major project evaluations reviewed.</Item>
  <Item>Most recent DHS / MICS / national census reviewed.</Item>
  <Item>Relevant UNFPA centralized evaluations reviewed.</Item>
  <Item>First-round interview list drafted: CO senior management + programme leads + key ERG members + selected stakeholders.</Item>
  <Item>Interview guides for the first round prepared, focused on understanding intervention logic.</Item>
</Checklist>

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Theory of change">The articulated causal chain — UNFPA inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → transformative results — with the assumptions and external factors at each step.</Term>
  <Term name="CPD">Country Programme Document — the master document approved by the UNFPA Executive Board defining the country programme cycle.</Term>
  <Term name="COAR">Country Office Annual Report — annual self-reported progress against AWP.</Term>
  <Term name="MTR">Mid-Term Review — a programme-level review at the midpoint of the country programme cycle; an essential prior input to the CPE.</Term>
  <Term name="Centralized evaluations">UNFPA-wide evaluations of thematic areas, partnerships, or modalities — published by the Independent Evaluation Office. Relevant ones for the country thematic scope must be reviewed.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
