<Callout variant="key" title="Illustrative, not statistical">
The evaluators should **not aim at a statistically representative sample, but rather an illustrative sample**. Sampling is purposive: it is designed to cover the variation in interventions, partners, sites, and rights-holders in a way that allows credible findings — not to enable population-level statistical inference.
</Callout>

At the start of the design phase, the CPE Manager provides the evaluation team with:
- A detailed overview of all interventions UNFPA supports (annex C of CPE ToR — lesson 1.8).
- The locations of these interventions.
- The stakeholders directly or indirectly involved, and those affected by implementation (annex B — lesson 1.9).

The sampling builds on the **stakeholder map** attached to the CPE ToR (annex B). It also draws on the evaluation team's documentary review and first round of interviews with the CO management and staff (lesson 2.1.2). The sampling may include stakeholders not initially listed in the CPE ToR annex B that the evaluators consider important.

<h3>Table 7 — the nine stakeholder selection criteria</h3>

<Checklist storageKey="ch2-l6-stakeholder-criteria" title="Stakeholder selection criteria (Table 7)">
  <Item>**All types of stakeholders relevant to each output/outcome** — ministries and administrative entities, implementing partners, execution agencies, donors, rights-holders (including the most vulnerable and furthest behind), academia, and CSOs. In the case of a humanitarian component, include cluster/sector leads and members.</Item>
  <Item>**Stakeholders involved in seemingly well-performing AND poorly performing interventions** — sampling only "success stories" produces biased findings.</Item>
  <Item>**For each output/outcome, stakeholders associated with ongoing UNFPA interventions AND with interventions already completed** during the cycle.</Item>
  <Item>**Stakeholders related to interventions in the capital AND in other regions / provinces / districts.**</Item>
  <Item>**Stakeholders associated with both financially large AND financially modest AWPs.**</Item>
  <Item>**Stakeholders associated with both tested interventions AND pilot interventions.**</Item>
  <Item>**Stakeholders associated with soft-aid activities** carried out by the CO.</Item>
  <Item>**Stakeholders who work in the same field as UNFPA** (e.g. SRHR, maternal health, SGBV) **but who are NOT directly partnering with UNFPA, nor implementing partners** — essential for triangulation.</Item>
  <Item>**Whenever relevant, stakeholders that have been involved with interagency projects** — joint programmes, UN-wide thematic working groups.</Item>
</Checklist>

The selection criteria should apply to the **actual relationship of the stakeholders to UNFPA outputs and contribution to outcomes** — rather than being based on financially designated projects or activities.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Document the rationale">
Once the sampling is done, the evaluators — in a short narrative — must **explain the rationale for the selection** of the retained stakeholders. They must also explain why some groups or organisations have **not been included**, and the potential implications for the evaluation. The sample map is attached to the design report (**annex 3**).
</Callout>

<h3>Site selection</h3>

A UNFPA country programme is typically implemented in different locations. The evaluators will only be able to visit a few. Therefore, the team needs to develop **sound criteria for selecting** municipalities, districts, or provinces.

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Thematic diversity">The sample of sites should reflect the range of interventions UNFPA supports — thematic mix matching the country programme's outcome areas.</KV>

  <KV title="Contextual variation">Varying contexts in which interventions are implemented — urban/rural, conflict-affected/stable, high/low programme intensity.</KV>

  <KV title="Humanitarian inclusion">For CPEs with a humanitarian component, the selected sites should include locations where UNFPA carried out humanitarian intervention.</KV>

  <KV title="Documented exclusions">Where sites are excluded for access, security, or logistics reasons, the team must document and justify the exclusion.</KV>
</KVGrid>

In the design report, the team presents the criteria and the rationale for site selection.

<h3>Table 8 — the field-phase agenda</h3>

After the initial selection of stakeholders and sites, each evaluation team member — under the supervision of the team leader — fills a draft field-work agenda. The agenda is a table with columns for:

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Date / week">Calendar slot for the meeting.</KV>

  <KV title="Activity / institution">The organisation or partner being visited.</KV>

  <KV title="Person to meet">Specific name of the interviewee.</KV>

  <KV title="Position / role">Job title and role context.</KV>

  <KV title="Stakeholder category">Cluster: implementer, donor, decision-maker, rights-holder, sectoral non-UNFPA actor.</KV>

  <KV title="Link with CPD output and outcome">Which output/outcome the interview informs.</KV>

  <KV title="Site location">Where the interview takes place.</KV>

  <KV title="Telephone, email, notes">Contact details and logistical notes.</KV>
</KVGrid>

The team leader reviews, makes amendments, and consolidates the entries into the final field phase agenda — **annexed to the design report (annex 6)**.

<h3>What "completion" means at design</h3>

If the field-work agenda covers three weeks (duration determined by scope and the recourse to virtual interviews for some informants):

<KVGrid>
  <KV title="Weeks 1–2">**At least the first two weeks** of the agenda should be completed — with all meetings confirmed by the CPE Manager — at the end of the design phase.</KV>

  <KV title="Week 3">The third week will be finalised as implementation moves forward. Some flexibility is useful as cancellations and rescheduling are unavoidable.</KV>

  <KV title="Additional interviews">Additional interviews or follow-ups may be necessary based on the evaluators' assessment of data collected so far. Some may occur beyond the three-week agenda as the team enters analysis and report writing.</KV>
</KVGrid>

<InPractice country="From the handbook">
The process of developing the agenda is complex and time-consuming. It requires extensive back-and-forth between evaluators, CPE Manager, other CO staff, and implementing partners. The CPE Manager plays an instrumental role: drafting letters of request, sending invitations to selected stakeholders, seeking confirmation of their availability. Meanwhile, the team leader ensures different team members don't plan to meet the same people — consulting each other to rationalise and streamline team work.
</InPractice>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Protocol with senior government interviews">
Appropriate protocols must be followed with **government senior staff** (e.g. department or regional heads) for permission to interview staff under them. The selection of persons to interview may need to be made in consultation with the government.
</Callout>

<h3>Logistics ownership</h3>

As the sites are selected, the key informants identified, and the agenda consolidated, the **CPE Manager** is responsible for ensuring corresponding logistics are in place — with support from the YEE. All practical arrangements must be made well in advance of the start of the field phase to ensure smooth implementation (see lesson 3.1).

This is also the time for the CPE Manager and the CO communication officer to develop an **initial communication plan** to share the CPE results with internal and external audiences. They use the stakeholder map to define the preliminary target audience — to be revised as new opportunities for advocacy and communication are identified during implementation. The CPE Manager presents the initial communication plan, with a provisional budget, to the CO Representative for review and approval (lesson 5.2).

<KeyTerms>
  <Term name="Purposive sampling">Sampling intentionally for variation across stakeholders, interventions, sites — not random sampling. The CPE's standard.</Term>
  <Term name="Illustrative sample">A sample chosen to illustrate the range of UNFPA work and stakeholder experience — not to enable statistical inference.</Term>
  <Term name="Field-phase agenda">The week-by-week schedule of who the team will meet, where, and for what — finalised at the end of the design phase (weeks 1–2) with week 3 flexible.</Term>
  <Term name="Triangulation through outside voices">Including stakeholders working in the same field as UNFPA but NOT partnering with UNFPA — Table 7 criterion 8 — is what makes triangulation possible.</Term>
</KeyTerms>
